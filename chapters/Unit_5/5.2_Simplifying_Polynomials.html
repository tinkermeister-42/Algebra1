5.2 - Simplifying Polynomials

In the last lesson, you learned how exponents behave: how powers multiply, divide, and combine. Now we will use those same ideas to understand and simplify polynomials, the expressions that form the backbone of algebra.

When we simplify polynomials, we are really just doing what we have always done: organize, combine, and clean things up, but now with variables and exponents.

binomial, degree, like terms, monomial, polynomial, standard form, trinomial

  1. Simplify \(3(2x - 4) + 5x - 7\).

  2. Evaluate \(4x^2 - 2x + 7 - x^2\) when \(x = -3\).

  3. Rewrite \(x^{-3} \cdot x^5\) using only positive exponents.

5.2.1 - Understanding Polynomials

A polynomial is a sum or difference of terms. Each term includes a coefficient (a number), a variable, and a whole number exponent.

Every term like \(7x^3\), \(-4x^2\), or \(5\) follows this pattern.

The number \(5\) is a constant, but it still fits the rule because we can think of it as \(5x^0\).
Since any number to the zero power equals 1, \(5x^0\) is the same as \(5\).

These are polynomials:

  • \(3x^2 + 2x - 5\)
  • \(7y^3 - 4y\)
  • \(9\)

These are not polynomials:

  • \(\dfrac{2}{x}\) (variable in the denominator → negative exponent)
  • \(x^{-3} + 4\) (negative exponent)
  • \(\sqrt{x} + 5\) (fractional exponent)

We only allow whole number exponents so the variable never ends up in a denominator or under a root.

Some polynomials have special names based on the number of terms they contain.

Number of Terms Name Example
1

monomial

 \(7x^3\)
2

binomial

 \(4x + 1\)
3

trinomial

 \(x^2 + 3x + 2\)

Polynomials with more than three terms do not have a special name. We just call them polynomials.

The exponent on a term is called its degree. Polynomials have a degree as well. The degree of a polynomial is the largest exponent in the expression.

Example 1: A matter of degree

Find the special name and degree of each of the following polynomial:

  • \(5z^6 - 2z^2 - 3\)
  • \(2\)
  • \(3x - 1\)

All we have to do is count the terms and find the largest exponent.

Polynomial Special Name Degree
\(5z^6 - 2z^2 - 3\) trinomial 6
\(2\) monomial 0
\(3x - 1\) binomial 1

Polynomials can get quite large and messy. To keep polynomials organized, we usually write them in what is called standard form. This means arranging the terms from highest degree to lowest.

Writing in standard form does not change the value of the expression, but it makes it easier to read, compare, and simplify. When every polynomial follows the same order, it is also easier to spot patterns.

Example 2: Standardizing polynomials

Write each of the following polynomials in standard form:

  1. \(2y^2 - 3y^3 - 3\)
  2. \(2 - 3x + 2x^2\)
  3. \(9b^2 + 3b^3 - b^4 + 3\)

We just need to sort the polynomials from largest to smallest exponent.

Standard Form
a \(-3y^3 + 2y^2 - 3\)
b \(2x^2 - 3x + 2\)
c \(-b^4 + 3b^3 + 9b^2 + 3\)

For each of the following polynomials:

  • Determine if it has a special name.
  • Write the polynomial in standard form.
  • Identify the degree of the polynomial

  1. \(3 - 2x^4\)

  2. \(-5x^7 + 2x - 6\)

  3. \(42\)

  4. \(\tfrac{1}{3}a^9 - \tfrac{2}{9}a^3 + \tfrac{3}{5}a^7 - 4\)

Special Name Standard Form Degree
1 binomial \(-2x^4 + 3\) 4
2 trinomial \(-5x^7 + 2x - 6\) 7
3 monomial \(42\) 0
4 None \(\tfrac{1}{3}a^9 + \tfrac{3}{5}a^7 - 2a^3 - 4\) 9

Once we can recognize and name polynomials, the next step is to simplify them.
Simplifying makes expressions shorter, cleaner, and easier to work with.
We will use the exponent and combining rules from last time to tidy up polynomial expressions.

5.2.2 - Simplifying Polynomials

Now that you can distribute, combine like terms, and use exponent rules, you’re ready to turn messy expressions into clean polynomials in standard form. This is an essential skill before we start multiplying and factoring polynomials.

The process is always the same:

  1. Distribute if parentheses are present.
  2. Apply exponent rules when needed.
  3. Combine like terms.
  4. Rewrite the answer in standard form (highest power to lowest).

Example 3: Combining like terms

Simplify \(3x^2 + 5x^2 - 4x\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 3x^2 + 5x^2 - 4x &= (3x^2 + 5x^2) - 4x \\ &= 8x^2 - 4x \end{aligned} \]

Example 4: Adding two polynomials

Simplify \((2x^3 + 4x^2) + (x^3 - 5x)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} (2x^3 + 4x^2) + (x^3 - 5x) &= 2x^3 + 4x^2 + x^3 - 5x \\ &= (2x^3 + x^3) + 4x^2 - 5x \\ &= 3x^3 + 4x^2 - 5x \end{aligned} \]

Example 5: Distributing a monomial

Simplify \(3x(2x^2 + 5x - 4)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 3x(2x^2 + 5x - 4) &= 3x \cdot 2x^2 + 3x \cdot 5x - 3x \cdot 4 \\ &= 6x^3 + 15x^2 - 12x \end{aligned} \]

Example 6: Multiple steps with subtraction

Simplify \(3(2x^2 - x + 5) - (x^2 + 4x - 1)\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 3(2x^2 - x + 5) - (x^2 + 4x - 1) &= (6x^2 - 3x + 5\cdot3) - (x^2 + 4x - 1) \\ &= 6x^2 - 3x + 15 - x^2 - 4x + 1 \\ &= (6x^2 - x^2) + (-3x - 4x) + (15 + 1) \\ &= 5x^2 - 7x + 16 \end{aligned} \]

\[ \begin{aligned} -3x(2 - 5x - x^2) + 4x - 1 &= -6x + 15x^2 + 3x^3 + 4x - 1 \\ &= -2x + 15x^2 + 3x^3 - 1 \\ &= 3x^3 + 15x^2 - 2x - 1 \end{aligned} \]
