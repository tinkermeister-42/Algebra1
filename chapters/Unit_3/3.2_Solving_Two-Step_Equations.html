3.2 - Solving Two-Step Equations

With one-step equations, we identified the operation and did the opposite to both sides. Two-step equations are the same idea with one new decision to make. Which operation do we deal with first?

equation, inverse operations, solution, variable

🔥 Warm Up

  1. Solve \(5x = 15\) for \(x\). Check your solution.

  2. Solve \(x - 3 = 10\) for \(x\). Check your solution.

  3. A carnival charges $5 for entry and $2 per ride. Alice paid a total of $35. Write an equation to find the number of rides Alice went on.

3.2.1 - Two-Step Equations

We solve equations by getting the variable by itself. With one operation, we solve by doing the opposite. When there are two operations, we do the same thing one operation at a time. To decide which comes first, we apply the order of operations in reverse.

When you simplify expressions, follow PEMDAS. When you solve, go the other direction.

Example 1: How much does an apple weigh?

Here we have 2 apples and one weight on the left-hand side and 7 weights on the other. Let’s find a, the weight of one apple.

\[ 2a + 1 = 7 \]

Following the order of operations (PEMDAS) in reverse, we start by undoing the addition.

\[ 2a + 1 \textcolor{red}{-1} = 7 \textcolor{red}{-1} \]

Now we are ready to undo the multiplication.

\[ \begin{aligned} 2a \textcolor{red}{\div 2} &= 6 \textcolor{red}{\div 2} \\ a &= 3 \end{aligned} \]

An apple has a weight of 3.

Here are the steps showing just the algebra.

\[ \begin{aligned} 2a + 1 &= 7 \\ \\ 2a + 1 \textcolor{red}{-1} &= 7 \textcolor{red}{-1} \snminus{1} \\ 2a &= 6 \\ \\ 2a \textcolor{red}{\div 2} &= 6 \textcolor{red}{\div 2} \sndiv{2} \\ a &= 3 \end{aligned} \]

\[ \begin{aligned} 3x - 2 &= 7 \\ \\ 3x - 2 \textcolor{red}{+2} &= 7 \textcolor{red}{+2} \\ 3x &= 9 \\ \\ 3x \textcolor{red}{\div 3} &= 9 \textcolor{red}{\div 3} \\ x &= 3 \end{aligned} \]

Example 2: Solving with division

Solve \(\frac{x}{8} - 3 = 7\).

This works the same way. First we undo the subtraction. Then we undo the division.

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{x}{8} - 3 &= 7 \\ \\ \frac{x}{8} - 3 \textcolor{red}{+3} &= 7 \textcolor{red}{+3} \snplus{3} \\ \frac{x}{8} &= 10 \\ \\ \frac{x}{8} \textcolor{red}{\times 8} &= 10 \textcolor{red}{\times 8} \sntimes{8} \\ x &= 80 \end{aligned} \]

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{x}{2} + 6 &= 10 \\ \\ \frac{x}{2} + 6 \textcolor{red}{-6} &= 10 \textcolor{red}{-6} \\ \frac{x}{2} &= 4 \\ \\ \frac{x}{2} \textcolor{red}{\times 2} &= 4 \textcolor{red}{\times 2} \\ x &= 8 \end{aligned} \]

Example 3: Negative coefficients

Solve \(-x + 5 = 3\).

\[ \begin{aligned} -x + 5 &= 3 \\ \\ -x + 5 \textcolor{red}{-5} &= 3 \textcolor{red}{-5} \snminus{5} \\ -x &= -2 \\ \\ -x \textcolor{red}{\div -1} &= -2 \textcolor{red}{\div -1} \sndiv{-1} \\ x &= 2 \end{aligned} \]

When a variable has no coefficient, the coefficient is \(1\). When the variable has only a minus sign, the coefficient is \(-1\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 3 - x &= 7 \\ \\ 3 \textcolor{red}{-3} - x &= 7 \textcolor{red}{-3} \snminus{3} \\ -x &= 4 \\ \\ -x \textcolor{red}{\div -1} &= 4 \textcolor{red}{\div -1} \sndiv{-1} \\ x &= -4 \end{aligned} \]

3.2.2 - Word Problems with Two-Step Equations

Equations are especially useful when a situation involves two operations. Let’s look at some examples.

Example 4: Babysitting

A babysitter charges $10 for travel plus $8 per hour. The total charge was $42. How many hours did the babysitter work?

Let \(h\) be the number of hours worked.

The following equation can be used to model this situation.

\[ 8h + 10 = 42 \]

Now we solve for \(h\):

\[ \begin{aligned} 8h + 10 \textcolor{red}{-10} &= 42 \textcolor{red}{-10} \snminus{10} \\ 8h &= 32 \\ \\ 8h \textcolor{red}{\div 8} &= 32 \textcolor{red}{\div 8} \sndiv{8} \\ h &= 4 \end{aligned} \]

The babysitter worked 4 hours.

Example 5: Cellphone plan

A phone plan charges $25 each month plus $0.05 per text. Last month the bill was $32. How many texts were sent?

Let \(t\) be the number of texts sent.

The equation we use to model this is:

\[ 0.05t + 25 = 32 \]

Now we solve for \(t\):

\[ \begin{aligned} 0.05t + 25 \textcolor{red}{-25} &= 32 \textcolor{red}{-25} \snminus{25} \\ 0.05t &= 7 \\ \\ 0.05t \textcolor{red}{\div 0.05} &= 7 \textcolor{red}{\div 0.05} \sndiv{0.05} \\ t &= 140 \end{aligned} \]

There were 140 texts sent.

Example 6: Connections to geometry

The perimeter of a rectangle is 28cm. The length is 2 more than twice the width. Find the width of the rectangle.

Let \(W\) be the width of the rectangle.

An equation that models this situation is:

\[ \begin{aligned} 2W + 2L &= 28 \stepnote{perimeter formula} \\ \\ 2W + 2(2W + 2) &= 28 \stepnote{substitute and simplify}\\ 2W + 4W + 4 &= 28 \\ \\ 6W + 4 &= 28 \end{aligned} \]

Now we can solve for \(W\):

\[ \begin{aligned} 6W + 4 \textcolor{red}{-4} &= 28 \textcolor{red}{-4} \snminus{4} \\ 6W &= 24 \\ \\ 6W \textcolor{red}{\div 6} &= 24 \textcolor{red}{\div 6} \sndiv{6} \\ W &= 4 \end{aligned} \]

The width of the rectangle is 4cm.

A gym membership is $20 plus $15 per class. Your total cost was $65. How many classes did you attend?

Let \(c\) be the number of classes attended.

The equation \(15c + 20 = 65\) can be used to model this situation.

\[ \begin{aligned} 15c + 20 \textcolor{red}{-20} &= 65 \textcolor{red}{-20} \snminus{20} \\ 15c &= 45 \\ \\ 15c \textcolor{red}{\div 15} &= 45 \textcolor{red}{\div 15} \sndiv{15} \\ c &= 3 \end{aligned} \]

You attended 3 classes.
