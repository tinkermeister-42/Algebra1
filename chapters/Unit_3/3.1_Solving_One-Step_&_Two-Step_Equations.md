# 3.1 – Solving One- and Two-Step Equations {.unnumbered}

In Unit 2 you learned how to **simplify** and **evaluate** expressions. Now we turn expressions into **equations** and solve them like puzzles. We’ll use the idea of **balance** and **inverse operations** to isolate the variable and **check** our answers. No graphing yet—just clean algebra moves.


:::{.objectives}

* [ ] I can solve **one-step equations** using inverse operations (add/subtract, multiply/divide).
* [ ] I can solve **two-step equations** and **check** solutions by substitution.
* [ ] I can show each step clearly to keep both sides **balanced**.
:::

### 3.1.1 – One-Step Equations: Add / Subtract

Think “keep the scale balanced.” Do the **same** thing to **both** sides.

**Example A**
Solve $p+7=13$

$$
\begin{align*}
p+7&=13 \\
p + 7 \textcolor{red}{-7} &= 13 \textcolor{red}{-7} \\
p &= 6
\end{align*}
$$

**Check:** $6+7=13$ ✓

**Example B**
Solve $m-12=-5$

$$
m-12+12=-5+12\;\Rightarrow\;m=7
$$

:::{.gotcha title="Same move to both sides"}
Whatever you do to one side, do to the other—**and to every term** on that side.
:::

:::{.you-try-m title="Solve"}

1. $y-9=-2$

2. $a+3.5=-1.2$

:::

:::{.answer title="Answer"}

1. $y=7$

2. $a=-4.7$
   
:::

---

### 3.1.2 – One-Step Equations: Multiply / Divide

Undo multiplication with **division**. Undo division with **multiplication**.

**Example C**
Solve $5k=40$ $\Rightarrow$ $k=\tfrac{40}{5}=8$

**Example D**
Solve $\dfrac{r}{4}=-6$ $\Rightarrow$ $r=4(-6)=-24$

**Example E**
Solve $\tfrac{1}{5}t=9$ $\Rightarrow$ multiply both sides by 5: $t=45$

:::{.gotcha title="Clear tiny fractions early"}
If you see $\tfrac{1}{n}u=c$, multiply both sides by $n$ first. It keeps numbers simple.
:::

:::{.you-try-m title="Solve"}

1. $-3q=27$

2. $\dfrac{s}{-3}=6$

3. $\tfrac{1}{4}w=7$

:::{.answer title="Answer"}

1. $q=-9$ 
   
2. $s=-18$ 

3. $w=28$

:::

---

### 3.1.3 – Two-Step Equations (Undo Order)

First undo **addition/subtraction**. Then undo **multiply/divide**.

**Example F**
Solve $2b+7=25$

$$
2b+7-7=25-7\Rightarrow 2b=18 \Rightarrow b=\tfrac{18}{2}=9
$$

**Example G**
Solve $\dfrac{n}{6}+5=3$

$$
\dfrac{n}{6}+5-5=3-5\Rightarrow \dfrac{n}{6}=-2 \Rightarrow n=6(-2)=-12
$$

**Example H (with negatives)**
Solve $-4z+9=1$

$$
-4z=1-9=-8 \Rightarrow z=\tfrac{-8}{-4}=2
$$

:::{.you-try-m title="Try these"}

1. $3m-4=17$

2. $\dfrac{u}{3}-4=-10$

:::

:::{.answer title="Answer"}

1. $m=7$ 

2. $u=-18$
   
:::

---

### 3.1.4 – Check Your Solution

Plug your answer back into the **original** equation.

**Example I**
Does $p=11$ solve $0.2p+1.6=3.8$?

$$
0.2(11)+1.6=2.2+1.6=3.8 \quad \checkmark
$$

:::{.think title="Why check?"}
A quick check catches sign slips and missed steps. It also builds confidence.
:::

---

### 3.1.5 – From Words to Equations (Quick Practice)

* “Seven less than a number is $-2$.” $\;\rightarrow\; t-7=-2$
* “One third of a number equals 12.” $\;\rightarrow\; \tfrac{1}{3}g=12$

:::{.you-try-m title="Write and solve"}

1. “Twice a number plus 5 is $-13$.” (use $r$)

2. “A number divided by 4 minus 2 equals 6.” (use $v$)

:::

:::{.answer title="Answer"}

1. $2r+5=-13 \Rightarrow r=-9$ 
2. $\tfrac{v}{4}-2=6 \Rightarrow v=32$

:::
