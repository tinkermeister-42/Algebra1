# 3.1 – Solving One- and Two-Step Equations {.unnumbered}

In Unit 2, we learned how to work with {{< glink equivalent expressions >}}. Sometimes we could check a number, {{< glink substitute >}} it into both expressions, and see that they matched. But, with some expressions, we noticed something important:

Some values worked.

Other values didn’t.

This brings us to **equations**. In this unit, we do the opposite: instead of trying numbers to check if two expressions are equal, we will learn to **find the numbers that make them equal**.

Those values are called the {{< glink solutions >}} of the equation.

In this lesson, we begin with the basic rules for solving equations:

- Keep both sides equal.

- Undo operations step by step until the variable is by itself.

Later lessons will build on these rules to solve more complicated equations.

:::{.objectives}

- [ ] I can solve one-step equations and show my work.
- [ ] I can solve two-step equations and show my work.
- [ ] I can check a solution by substituting it into the original equation.
:::

### 3.1.1 – One-Step Equations (Addition and Subtraction)

An {{< glink equation >}} is made up of two expressions separated by an equals sign. Our goal is to find values of the variables in each expression that make the equation true. 

You can think of it like a scale with an expression in each pan. When the value of the left-hand side (LHS) is the same as the right-hand side (RHS), the scale will be in balance.

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

---

**Example 1:** One-step equations with addition

Here is a scale with an apple and 2 weights on the left-hand side and with 5 weights on the right-hand side. We would like to know the weight of an apple.

Let *a* stand for the weight of one apple.

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance_x_plus_2_equal_5_1.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

$$
a + 2 = 5
$$

To find the weight (**a**) of the apple. We need to get it by itself on one of the pans with weights on the other side. If we do that, and the scale is still in balance, we will know the weight of the apple.

Start by taking the two weights off the left-hand side.

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance_x_plus_2_equal_5_2.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

$$
a + 2 \textcolor{red}{- 2} = 5
$$

By removing the weights from the left pan, we got the apple by itself, but now the right pan weighs more than the left. We have to take two weights off of the right pan to restore the balance.

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance_x_plus_2_equal_5_3.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

$$
\begin{align*}
a &= 5 \textcolor{red}{- 2} \\
a &= 3
\end{align*}
$$

The weight of the apple is **3**.

To {{< glink solve >}} the equation, we only needed one step -- subtract 2 from each side. We always **undo addition by subtracting**.

We don't usually draw pictures to solve equations. Here is what the solutions looks like showing just the algebra.

$$
\begin{align*}
a+2 &= 5 \\
a+2 \textcolor{red}{- 2} &= 5 \textcolor{red}{- 2} \tag*{\class{stepnote}{\textcolor{gray}{-2 both sides}}} \\
a &= 3
\end{align*}
$$

:::{.you-try title="Solve $x + 9 = 11$."}

$$
\begin{align*}
x + 9 &= 11 \\
x + 9 \textcolor{red}{- 9} &= 11 \textcolor{red}{- 9} \\
x &= 2
\end{align*}
$$

:::

---

**Example 2:** One-step equations with subtraction

Solving equations involving subtraction works the same way with a single difference. We always **undo subtraction by adding**.

Solve the equation $x - 5 = 8$.

$$
\begin{align*}
x - 5 &= 8 \\
x - 5 \textcolor{red}{+ 5} &= 8 \textcolor{red}{+ 5} \tag*{\class{stepnote}{\textcolor{gray}{+5 both sides}}} \\
x &= 13
\end{align*}
$$

:::{.you-try title="Solve $x - 12 = 2$."}

$$
\begin{align*}
x - 12 &= 2 \\
x - 12 \textcolor{red}{+ 12} &= 2 \textcolor{red}{+ 12} \\
x &= 14
\end{align*}
$$

:::

---

### 3.1.2 -- One-Step Equations (Multiplication and Division)

Solving one-step equations with multiplication and division is very similar to solving them with addition and subtraction.

**Example 3:** One-step Equations with Multiplication

Let's return to our scale with apples and weights. This time, there are 2 apples on the left-hand side and 6 weights on the right. We want to know the weight of one apple. 

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance_2x_equal_6_1.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

$$
2a = 6
$$

We can see that two apples is the same as 6 weights. Notice that for each apple there are 3 weights.

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance_2x_equal_6_2.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

We get an apple by itself by **dividing**. Like we saw with addition and subtraction, we can't divide just one side because it won't stay balanced.

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance_2x_equal_6_3.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

$$
2a \textcolor{red}{\div 2} = 6
$$

We have to **divide both sides** by 2 to maintain the balance.

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance_2x_equal_6_4.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

$$
\begin{align*}
2a \textcolor{red}{\div 2} &= 6 \textcolor{red}{\div 2} \\
a &= 3
\end{align*}
$$

An apple has a weight of **3**.

Here is the solution showing just the algebra.

$$
\begin{align*}
2a &= 6 \\
2a \textcolor{red}{\div 2} &= 6 \textcolor{red}{\div 2} \tag*{\class{stepnote}{\textcolor{gray}{÷2 both sides}}} \\
a &= 3
\end{align*}
$$

:::{.you-try title="Solve $3x = 15$."}
$$
\begin{align*}
3x &= 15 \\
3x \textcolor{red}{\div 3} &= 15 \textcolor{red}{\div 3} \\
x &= 5
\end{align*}
$$
:::

---

**Example 4:** One-step Equations With Division

Solving one-step equations with division is the same. But instead of dividing, we **multiply**.

Solve $\frac{a}{3} = 1$.

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{a}{3} &= 1 \\
\frac{a}{3} \textcolor{red}{\times 3} &= 1 \textcolor{red}{\times 3} \tag*{\class{stepnote}{\textcolor{gray}{×3 both sides}}} \\
a &= 3
\end{align*}
$$

:::{.you-try title="Solve $\frac{x}{5}=10$"}
$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{x}{5} &= 10 \\
\frac{x}{5} \textcolor{red}{\times 5} &= 10 \textcolor{red}{\times 5} \\
x &= 50
\end{align*}
$$
:::


:::{.remember title="How to Undo Operations"}
Do the same move to **both** sides.

| If you see… | Undo with… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| $+$ | $-$ | $x+7=12$ &rarr; $x=5$ |
| $-$ | $+$ | $x-9=4$ &rarr; $x=13$ |
| $\times$ | $\div$ | $4x=20$ &rarr; $x=5$ |
| $\div$ | $\times$ | $\tfrac{x}{3}=6$ &rarr; $x=18$ |

:::

### 3.1.3 – Two-Step Equations

Solving equations with more than one step works the same way as one-step equations, but we have to solve them by reversing the order of operations. 

**Example 5:** Two-step Equation With Addition and Multiplication

Here we have 2 apples and one weight on the left-hand side and 7 weights on the other.

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance_2x_plus_1_equal_7_1.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

$$
2a + 1 = 7
$$

Following the order of operations ({{< glink PEMDAS >}}) in reverse, we start by undoing the addition.

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance_2x_plus_1_equal_7_3.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

$$
2a + 1 \textcolor{red}{- 1} = 7 \textcolor{red}{- 1}
$$

Now we are ready to undo the multiplication.

![](/images/Unit_3/Lesson_1/balance_2x_plus_1_equal_7_5.png){width="300px" fig-align="center"}

$$
\begin{align*}
2a \textcolor{red}{\div 2} &= 6 \textcolor{red}{\div 2} \\
a &= 3
\end{align*}
$$

An apple has a weight of **3**.

Here are the steps showing just the algebra.

$$
\begin{align*}
2a + 1 &= 7 \\
2a + 1 \textcolor{red}{- 1} &= 7 \textcolor{red}{- 1} \tag*{\class{stepnote}{\textcolor{gray}{-1 both sides}}} \\
2a \textcolor{red}{\div 2} &= 6 \textcolor{red}{\div 2} \tag*{\class{stepnote}{\textcolor{gray}{÷2 both sides}}} \\
a &= 3
\end{align*}
$$

:::{.you-try title="Solve $3x - 2 = 7$."}
$$
\begin{align*}
3x - 2 &= 7 \\
3x - 2 \textcolor{red}{+ 2} &= 7 \textcolor{red}{+ 2} \\
3x \textcolor{red}{\div 3} &= 9 \textcolor{red}{\div 3} \\
x &= 3
\end{align*}
$$
:::

**Example 6:** Two-step Equation with Division and Subtraction

Solve $\frac{x}{8} - 3 = 7$.

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{x}{8} - 3 &= 7 \\
\frac{x}{8} - 3 \textcolor{red}{+ 3} &= 7 \textcolor{red}{+ 3} \tag*{\class{stepnote}{\textcolor{gray}{+3 both sides}}} \\
\frac{x}{8} \textcolor{red}{\times 8} &= 10 \textcolor{red}{\times 8} \tag*{\class{stepnote}{\textcolor{gray}{×8 both sides}}} \\
x &= 80
\end{align*}
$$

:::{.you-try title="Solve $\frac{x}{2} + 6 = 10$."}

$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{x}{2} + 6 &= 10 \\
\frac{x}{2} + 6 \textcolor{red}{- 6} &= 10 \textcolor{red}{- 6} \\
\frac{x}{2} \textcolor{red}{\times 2} &= 4 \textcolor{red}{\times 2} \\
x &= 8
\end{align*}
$$

:::

## 3.1.3 – Checking a Solution ✅

You can always check your work by plugging the answer back in.

**Example – Check**
Solve $3x - 5 = 16$.

* Add 5: $3x = 21$
* Divide by 3: $x = 7$
* Check: $3(7) - 5 = 21 - 5 = 16$.
