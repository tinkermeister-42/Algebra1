3.3 - Multi-Step Equations with Distribution

In the last lessons, you learned how to solve one-step and two-step equations. Those were like solving simple puzzles: one or two moves and you had the answer.

But not every equation is that neat. Sometimes parentheses get in the way, or extra terms make things look messy. These are called multi-step equations.

In this lesson, you’ll learn how to simplify first (by using the distributive property and combining like terms) and then solve the equation step by step. We’ll also practice checking our solutions to be sure they really work.

combine like terms, distributive property, equation, inverse operations

🔥 Warm Up

  1. Solve \(3x - 5 = 19\)

  2. Simplify \(-2(3x - 4) + 2x\)

  3. Is \(x=10\) a solution to \(5(x - 1) = 45\)?

3.3.1 - Distribution in Equations

Sometimes equations have parentheses. When that happens, you can’t get the variable by itself right away, you have to simplify first. This is where the distributive property comes in.

Remember from Lesson 2.4:

\[ a(b + c) = ab + ac \]

That rule lets us “remove” parentheses so the equation is easier to work with.

Example 1:

Solve:

\[ 3(x + 4) = 21 \]

The variable \(x\) is trapped inside the parentheses. We start by applying the distributive property to free \(x\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \textcolor{red}{3}(x + 4) &= 21 \\ 3x + 12 &= 21 \stepnote{distribute the 3} \end{aligned} \]

Now we can solve the two-step equation.

\[ \begin{aligned} 3x + 12 \textcolor{red}{- 12} &= 21 \textcolor{red}{- 12} \snminus{12} \\ 3x &= 9 \\ \\ 3x \textcolor{red}{\div 3} &= 9 \textcolor{red}{\div 3} \sndiv{3} \\ x &= 3 \end{aligned} \]

Example 2:

Solve:

\[ -2(2x - 5) = 6 \]

Notice the negative sign. We need to be extra careful to include it when we distribute.

\[ \begin{aligned} \textcolor{red}{-2}(2x - 5) &= 6 \stepnote{distribute the -2} \\ -4x + 10 &= 6 \\ \\ -4x + 10 \textcolor{red}{- 10} &= 6 \textcolor{red}{- 10} \snminus{10} \\ -4x &= -4 \\ \\ -4x \textcolor{red}{\div -4} &= -4 \textcolor{red}{\div -4} \sndiv{-4} \\ x &= 1 \end{aligned} \]

Solve:

\[ \begin{aligned} \textcolor{red}{3}(2x + 1) &= 15 \stepnote{distribute the 3} \\ 6x + 3 &= 15 \\ \\ 6x + 3 \textcolor{red}{-3} &= 15 \textcolor{red}{-3} \snminus{3} \\ 6x &= 12 \\ \\ 6x \textcolor{red}{\div 6} &= 12 \textcolor{red}{\div 6} \sndiv{6} \\ x &= 2 \end{aligned} \]

3.3.2 - Combining Like Terms in Equations

Sometimes an equation looks messy because there are like terms on the same side of the equals sign. Before you can solve, you need to combine them into a single term.

Example 1: Combining like terms

Solve:

\[ 4x + 7 + 2x = 31 \]

Start by combining the like terms \(4x\) and \(2x\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \textcolor{blue}{4x} + 7 + \textcolor{blue}{2x} &= 31 \stepnote{combine like terms} \\ \textcolor{blue}{6x} + 7 &= 31 \end{aligned} \]

Now solve the two-step equation.

\[ \begin{aligned} 6x + 7 \textcolor{red}{- 7} &= 31 \textcolor{red}{- 7} \snminus{7} \\ 6x &= 24 \\ \\ 6x \textcolor{red}{\div 6} &= 24 \textcolor{red}{\div 6} \sndiv{6} \\ x &= 4 \end{aligned} \]

Example 2: Distribution and like terms

Solve:

\[ 2(x + 5) + 3x = 25 \]

This equation has both parentheses and like terms. Start by distributing.

\[ \begin{aligned} \textcolor{red}{2}(x + 5) + 3x &= 25 \stepnote{distribute the 2} \\ 2x + 10 + 3x &= 25 \end{aligned} \]

Now combine \(2x\) and \(3x\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \textcolor{blue}{2x} + 10 + \textcolor{blue}{3x} &= 25 \stepnote{combine like terms} \\ \textcolor{blue}{5x} + 10 &= 25 \end{aligned} \]

Finish solving:

\[ \begin{aligned} 5x + 10 \textcolor{red}{- 10} &= 25 \textcolor{red}{- 10} \snminus{10} \\ 5x &= 15 \\ \\ 5x \textcolor{red}{\div 5} &= 15 \textcolor{red}{\div 5} \sndiv{5} \\ x &= 3 \end{aligned} \]

Example 3: Distribution and like terms with negatives

Solve:

\[ -3(x - 4) + 2x - 3 = 7 \]

Be careful with those negative signs!

\[ \begin{aligned} \textcolor{red}{-3}(x - 4) + 2x - 3 &= 7 \stepnote{distribute the -3} \\ -3x + 12 + 2x - 3 &= 7 \\ \\ \textcolor{blue}{-3x} + \textcolor{green}{12} + \textcolor{blue}{2x} \textcolor{green}{- 3} &= 7 \stepnote{combine like terms} \\ \textcolor{blue}{-x} + \textcolor{green}{9} &= 7 \\ \\ -x + 9 \textcolor{red}{-9} &= 7 \textcolor{red}{- 9} \snminus{9} \\ -x &= -2 \\ \\ -x \textcolor{red}{\div -1} &= -2 \textcolor{red}{\div -1} \sndiv{-1} \\ x &= 2 \end{aligned} \]

\[ \begin{aligned} \textcolor{red}{4}(x + 2) - 3x + 5 &= 18 \stepnote{distribute the 4} \\ 4x + 8 - 3x + 5 &= 18 \\ \\ \textcolor{blue}{4x} + \textcolor{green}{8} + \textcolor{blue}{-3x} + \textcolor{green}{5} &= 18 \stepnote{combine like terms} \\ \textcolor{blue}{x} + \textcolor{green}{13} &= 18 \\ \\ x + 13 \textcolor{red}{-13} &= 18 \textcolor{red}{-13} \snminus{13} \\ x &= 5 \end{aligned} \]

3.3.3 - Word Problems with Multi-Step Equations

Some problems lead to equations that look “messier” because they include parentheses or more than one set of like terms. We solve them the same way, by simplifying first, then isolating the variable.

Example 4: Selling t-shirts

A club sells T-shirts for $12 each. The total cost to make the shirts is $60 plus $6 per shirt. If the group made a profit of $84, how many shirts did they sell?

Let \(s\) be the number of shirts sold.

Total profit = money in − money out:

\[ 12s - (60 + 6s) = 84 \]

Simplify and solve:

\[ \begin{aligned} \textcolor{blue}{12s} - 60 \textcolor{blue}{- 6s} &= 84 \stepnote{combine like terms}\\ \textcolor{blue}{6s} - 60 &= 84 \\ \\ 6s - 60 \textcolor{red}{+ 60} &= 84 \textcolor{red}{+ 60} \snplus{60} \\ 6s &= 144 \\ \\ 6s \textcolor{red}{\div 6} &= 144 \textcolor{red}{\div 6} \sndiv{6} \\ s &= 24 \end{aligned} \]

They sold 24 shirts.

Example 5: Connections to Geometry

The perimeter of a triangle is 54 cm. The sides measure \(2x + 3\), \(3x - 2\), and \(x + 5\). Find the value of \(x\).

Write an equation for the perimeter:

\[ (2x + 3) + (3x - 2) + (x + 5) = 54 \]

Simplify and solve:

\[ \begin{aligned} (\textcolor{blue}{2x} + \textcolor{green}{3}) + (\textcolor{blue}{3x} \textcolor{green}{- 2}) + (\textcolor{blue}{x} \textcolor{green}{+ 5}) &= 54 \stepnote{combine like terms} \\ \textcolor{blue}{6x} + \textcolor{green}{6} &= 54 \\ \\ 6x + 6 \textcolor{red}{- 6} &= 54 \textcolor{red}{- 6} \snminus{6} \\ 6x &= 48 \\ \\ 6x \textcolor{red}{\div 6} &= 48 \textcolor{red}{\div 6} \sndiv{6} \\ x &= 8 \end{aligned} \]

Each side can now be found using \(x = 8\).

A car wash charges $8 per car. Supplies cost $24 plus $2 per car. If the group earned $96 in profit, how many cars did they wash?

Let \(c\) be the number of cars washed.

\[ 8c - (24 + 2c) = 96 \]

Simplify and solve:

\[ \begin{aligned} \textcolor{blue}{8c} - 24 \textcolor{blue}{- 2c} &= 96 \stepnote{combine like terms} \\ \textcolor{blue}{6c} - 24 &= 96 \\ \\ 6c - 24 \textcolor{red}{+ 24} &= 96 \textcolor{red}{+ 24} \snplus{24} \\ 6c &= 120 \\ \\ 6c \textcolor{red}{\div 6} &= 120 \textcolor{red}{\div 6} \sndiv{6} \\ c &= 20 \end{aligned} \]

They washed 20 cars.
