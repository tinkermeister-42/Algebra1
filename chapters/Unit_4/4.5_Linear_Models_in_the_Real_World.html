4.5 – Linear Models in the Real World

Real life is full of linear patterns — pay that rises with hours worked, temperature that drops over time, or costs that increase as you buy more. Whenever one quantity changes at a steady rate, we can model it with a linear function.

A linear model has the same structure as \[y = mx + b,\] but we rename \(x\) and \(y\) to match the situation — like \(P(e)\) for price or \(T(t)\) for temperature.

4.5.1 – What the Parts of a Linear Model Tell Us

Linear models always have two key pieces:

  • The slope (\(m\)) shows how fast something changes.
  • The intercept (\(b\)) shows where it starts.

Let’s look at how these appear in real situations.

Example 1: Streaming Service (Positive Slope)

A streaming service charges $2 for every episode you rent, plus a monthly fee of $7.99. Write a linear function that models the total price.

Let \(e\) = episodes and \(P(e)\) = total price (dollars).

The slope is 2 per episode, and the intercept is 7.99 dollars.

\[ P(e) = 2e + 7.99 \]

\[ \begin{align*} P(10) &= 2(10) + 7.99 \\ &= 27.99 \end{align*} \]

It would cost $27.99 to rent 10 episodes.

Example 2: Cooling Soup (Negative Slope)

A cup of soup cools from 90 °C to 60 °C over 10 minutes. Assuming a steady rate of cooling:

\[ T(t) = -3t + 90 \]

Let \(t\) = time (min), and \(T(t)\) = temperature (°C).

The slope –3 means the soup’s temperature drops 3 °C each minute. The intercept 90 °C shows the starting temperature right after pouring.

\[ T(15) = -3(15) + 90 = 45 \]

After 15 minutes, the soup will be 45 °C.

Example 3: Taxi Ride (Intercept Focus)

A taxi charges $3 to start the ride and $2.25 per mile.

\[ C(d) = 2.25d + 3 \]

Let \(d\) = miles, and \(C(d)\) = total cost (dollars).

The intercept 3 means you pay $3 before moving — the base fare. The slope 2.25 shows the cost per mile once the ride begins.

Example 4: Babysitting Job (Putting It Together)

A babysitter earns $12 per hour plus a $10 transportation fee.

Let \(h\) = hours, and \(P(h)\) = total pay (dollars).

\[ P(h) = 12h + 10 \]

The slope 12 means pay increases $12 per hour worked. The intercept 10 is the starting amount earned before any hours.

\[ P(5) = 12(5) + 10 = 70 \]

The babysitter earns $70 total.

4.5.2 – How to Create a Linear Model

So far, you’ve interpreted models. Now let’s learn how to make your own from words or data.

A linear model always follows the pattern \[ \text{output} = (\text{rate of change})(\text{input}) + (\text{starting value}) \]

Step 1: Find What’s Changing

Look for words that describe a steady increase or decrease. That number is your slope (\(m\)).

Examples:

  • “$15 per hour” → \(m = 15\)
  • “drops 3 degrees each minute” → \(m = -3\)
  • “gains 5 points per week” → \(m = 5\)

Step 2: Find Where It Starts

What’s true when your input is 0? That number is the intercept (\(b\)).

Examples:

  • “base fee of $20” → \(b = 20\)
  • “starts at 100 points” → \(b = 100\)
  • “initial temperature 90 °C” → \(b = 90\)

Step 3: Write the Equation

Put both pieces together and use variables that make sense.

Example:

A car rental costs $50 per day plus $30 in fees. Let \(d\) = days rented, \(C(d)\) = total cost. \[C(d) = 50d + 30\]

Example: Gym Membership

A gym charges a one-time $30 fee plus $25 per month.

Let \(m\) = months, and \(C(m)\) = total cost.

  1. Slope = 25 (per month)
  2. Intercept = 30 (one-time fee)
  3. Model: \[C(m) = 25m + 30\]

\[ C(6) = 25(6) + 30 = 180 \]

After 6 months, the total cost is $180.

  • Include units in your reasoning.
  • Watch for negative slopes — they show decreases.
  • The starting value might not come first in the sentence.
  • Choose variable names that fit the situation.

4.5.3 – Seeing Patterns in Data

In real life, data points don’t line up perfectly, but they often follow a trend. A best-fit line shows that overall direction.

Example – Height vs. Age

Each dot shows one student’s age and height. The line through the middle shows the average growth pattern.

The positive slope means height generally increases with age. The intercept estimates the height at age 0 — the starting point at birth.

It would mean that as one variable increases, the other decreases — for example, temperature falling as time passes.

  • The slope tells how fast something changes.
  • The intercept tells where it starts.
  • Together they describe how one value depends on another.
  • A best-fit line shows that relationship when data aren’t exact.

✍️ Practice On Your Own

🔹 Part A – Interpret Models

  1. The equation \(T(h) = -2h + 70\) shows the temperature \(T\) after \(h\) hours.

    • What does the slope mean?
    • What does the intercept mean?

  2. \(P(n) = 5n + 20\) models parking costs.

    • How much does it cost to park for 3 hours?
    • What do the 5 and 20 represent?

🔹 Part B – Write Your Own Models

  1. A movie theater charges $10 per ticket plus a $3 service fee.

    • Write a model for total cost \(C(t)\), where \(t\) is tickets bought.

  2. A phone loses 8 % battery every hour starting at 100 %.

    • Write and interpret a model for remaining charge \(B(h)\).

  3. You start with $50 and save $12 per week.

    • Write a model and use it to find your total after 8 weeks.

🔹 Part C – Compare and Predict

  1. Two gyms have different pricing plans:

    • Plan A: \(C_1(m)=25m+30\)
    • Plan B: \(C_2(m)=20m+60\)
    • Which plan costs less at first? Which is cheaper after 10 months?

  2. A taxi charges $2.50 per mile plus $3 start fee. Another charges $3 per mile but no start fee.

    • Write both equations.
    • When do they cost the same?

🔹 Part D – Interpret Graphs

  1. A scatter plot shows weekly hours of study vs. test scores. The best-fit line rises from left to right.

    • What does the slope tell you?
    • What would a flat line mean?

  2. The line \(D(t)\) in the graph slopes downward.

    • What kind of relationship does that show between \(D\) and \(t\)?

Sample answers (not shown to students):

  1. Slope = –2 °C/hour → cooling rate; intercept = 70 °C start.
  2. \(P(3)=35\); slope = cost per hour; intercept = base fee.
  3. \(C(t)=10t+3\).
  4. \(B(h)=100-8h\) (if linear).
  5. \(S(w)=12w+50\), so \(S(8)=146\).
  6. Plan A starts cheaper, Plan B cheaper after 6 months.
  7. \(C_1=2.5x+3\), \(C_2=3x\) → same at \(x=6\).
