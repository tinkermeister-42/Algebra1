2.4 - The Distributive Property

  1. Distribute first:

    \[ \begin{align*} 4(2x + 3) - (2x + 3) &= \\ 4(2x) + 4(3) -1(2x) -1(3) &= \\ 8x + 12 - 2x - 3 &= 6x + 9 \end{align*} \]

    Factor first: \[ \begin{align*} 4(2x + 3) - (2x + 3) &= \\ (4 - 1) * (2x + 3) &= \\ 3 (2x + 3) & = 6x + 9 \end{align*} \]

    These are equivalent because the distributive property works both ways. We can expand by multiplying into parentheses or we can factor out by pulling out what’s common. In both cases, we are just rearranging the same multiplication and subtraction.

  2. You have to distribute twice.

    \[ \begin{align*} (3x - 1) * (2x + 3) &= \\ (3x - 1)(2x) + (3x - 1)(-1) &= \\ 6x^2 - 2x - 3x + 1 &= 6x^2 -5x + 1 \end{align*} \]

2.6 - Equivalent Expressions

  1. \(3(x+k)=3x+3k=3x+12 \Rightarrow k=4\).

  2. Factor out an \(x+2\) from \(E_1(x)\)

    \[ \begin{align*} E_1(x) &= (x+2)(4x-1) + 3(x+2) \\ &= (x+2)((4x - 1) + 3) \\ &= (x + 2) (4x + 2) \\ &= 2(x + 2)(2x + 1) \end{align*} \]

    The expressions are equivalent.

3.5 - Equations With No or Infinite Solutions

  1. Expand RHS: \(2kx + 5 = 2kx + 3k\). Subtract \(2kx\): \(5 = 3k\).

    • If \(k = \tfrac{5}{3}\) → one solution.
    • If no \(k\)? Actually, wait — check carefully: if \(k = 0\), LHS = 5, RHS = 0, so no solution.
    • For any other \(k\), equation reduces to \(5 = 3k\). That’s only true when \(k = \tfrac{5}{3}\). So: one solution if \(k = \tfrac{5}{3}\), no solution if \(k=0\), otherwise no solution as well (gifted students can refine the logic).

  2. \(3x + 6 + 4y = 2x - 2y + 10\). Simplify: \(x + 6y - 4 = 0\). So \(x = -6y + 4\). One solution in terms of \(y\).
