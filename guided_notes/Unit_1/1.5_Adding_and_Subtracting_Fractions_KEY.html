Warm-Up

1. Monique ate 1 slice of pepperoni and 2 slices of cheese. Both pizzas were cut into 8 equal slices. How much pizza did she eat?

Answer: 38    How did you know? the slices are the same size, so just add the numerators

2. Jack ate 1 slice of a pepperoni pizza cut into 3 pieces and 1 slice of a cheese pizza cut into 4 pieces. How much pizza did he eat?

Answer: 712    Why is this one harder? the pieces are different sizes, so they must be rewritten first

1.5.1Like Denominators

Monique ate 1 slice + 2 slices, and both pizzas were cut into 8. Every slice is the same size, so just count slices: 18 + 28 = 38.
The denominator says how big each piece is. It did not change, so it stays. The numerator counts the pieces, so that is what changes.
  • Keep the denominator.
  • Combine the numerators.
  • Last step of every problem: simplify.
Gotcha: numerators only
Do not combine the denominators. 27 + 37 is not 514.

1.5.2Unlike Denominators

Different denominators mean the pieces are different sizes. A common denominator is a number both denominators divide into evenly.
To find one: list the multiples of each and take the first number both lists show.

Multiples of 3: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15…

Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16…  →  common denominator: 12

Then divide to find what each fraction gets multiplied by: 12 ÷ 3 = 4 and 12 ÷ 4 = 3.
Gotcha: top and bottom
44 = 1, so multiplying by it does not change the value. Multiply only the bottom and you have changed the fraction into a different number.
EX 5. 13 + 14

1.5.3Adding & Subtracting Mixed Numbers

Why not combine the whole numbers and then the fractions? It breaks on 614 − 338, because 14 is smaller than 38 and you have to borrow. Turn them into fractions first instead.
  1. Convert each to an improper fraction: abc = a × c + bc
  2. Get a common denominator, then combine.
  3. Simplify. Convert back if you need to.
A whole number is a fraction over 1, so 8 = 81.
