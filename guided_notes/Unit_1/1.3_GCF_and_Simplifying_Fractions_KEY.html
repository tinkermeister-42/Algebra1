Warm-Up

1. Which number do you think has the most prime factors? What makes you think so?   20    30    45    53

Counting every prime factor: 20, 30 and 45 each have 3 (2 × 2 × 5, 2 × 3 × 5, 3 × 3 × 5). Counting different primes: 30 has 3, the most. 53 is prime, so it has only 1.

2. What is the largest number you think divides both 12 and 18 evenly? 6

3. Which fraction does not belong, and why?   1218    49    23    2436

49. The other three all simplify to 23.

1.3.1Finding the GCF Using Factor Trees

To find the greatest common factor (GCF), break both numbers into prime factors with factor trees. Circle the leaves they both share. Multiply the shared factors.
EX 1. Find the GCF of 36 and 60.

36

60

Shared factors: 2 × 2 × 3    GCF = 12

36 = 22 × 32    60 = 22 × 3 × 5

EX 2. Find the GCF of 45 and 180.your turn

45

180

Shared factors: 3 × 3 × 5    GCF = 45

45 = 32 × 5    180 = 22 × 32 × 5

1.3.2Using Factor Trees to Divide

For the GCF of 36 and 60, we did not circle every leaf. For 36 we left behind a 3. For 60 we left behind a 5. This means 36 ÷ 12 = 3 and 60 ÷ 12 = 5.
EX 3. Use factor trees to divide 90 ÷ 15. Cross out the factors of 15. Multiply what is left.

90

15

Left over: 3 × 2    90 ÷ 15 = 6

90 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 5    15 = 3 × 5    cross out 3 and 5

EX 4. Use factor trees to divide 480 ÷ 60.your turn

480

60

Left over: 2 × 2 × 2    480 ÷ 60 = 8

480 = 25 × 3 × 5    60 = 22 × 3 × 5

1.3.3Simplifying Fractions with the GCF

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and the denominator by their GCF. In simplest form, the numerator and denominator share no factors other than 1.
EX 5. Simplify 3660.  The GCF of 36 and 60 is 12.

3660 = 36 ÷ ___60 ÷ ___ = 35

Both blanks are the GCF, 12.

EX 6. Simplify 9015.

9015 = 90 ÷ 1515 ÷ 15 = 61 = 6

Gotcha: what if they share no factors?
Then the two numbers are relatively prime. The fraction cannot be simplified. Example: 9 = 3 × 3 and 38 = 2 × 19 share no factors, so 938 is already in simplest form.
EX 7. Write 3090 in simplest form.your turn

GCF = 30     3090 = 30 ÷ 3090 ÷ 30 = 13

1.3 GCF & Simplifying Fractions - Practice

GCF Practice

1. Find the GCF of each pair.

a.  20, 30  10

b.  36, 45  9

c.  18, 48  6

d.  30, 42  6

e.  50, 65  5

f.  72, 90  18

g.  81, 108  27

2. Two numbers have a GCF of 6. One of them is 18. Give two possibilities for the other.

For example, 12 or 30. Each is a multiple of 6, and 6 is the largest factor it shares with 18.

3. Two numbers have a GCF of 1. What does that mean? Give an example.

They share no factor but 1 (relatively prime), e.g. 8 and 15.

5. Can a fraction be simplified if the GCF is 1? Explain and give an example.

No. A GCF of 1 means it is already in simplest form, e.g. 49.

Word Problems

6. You have 72 juice boxes and 60 cookies. You want snack packs with the same ratio of each, using all the items.

a. Greatest number of snack packs? 12

b. How many of each per pack? 6 juice boxes and 5 cookies

7. A store bundles 108 pairs of socks and 144 shirts, same ratio in each bundle, no leftovers.

a. Greatest number of bundles? 36

b. Socks and shirts per bundle? 3 pairs of socks and 4 shirts

8. A painter mixes 84 ounces of red and 36 ounces of blue into identical jars.

a. Greatest number of jars with none left over? 12

b. Ounces of red and blue per jar? 7 oz red and 3 oz blue

Challenge

9. Two numbers multiply to make 180 and their GCF is 6. What could they be?

6 and 30. 6 × 30 = 180 and their GCF is 6.

10. A teacher has 150 pencils and 100 pens. Every gift bag has the same ratio of pencils to pens, and each bag has 2 more pencils than pens. She uses all the items. How many bags, and what goes in each?

150 : 100 simplifies to 3 : 2. A bag with 3 pencils and 2 pens has only 1 more pencil. Double it: 6 pencils and 4 pens, which is 2 more. 150 ÷ 6 = 25 bags (and 100 ÷ 4 = 25 ✓).
