Warm-Up

1. How many different pairs of whole numbers multiply to give 36?

5 pairs: 1×36, 2×18, 3×12, 4×9, 6×6

2. How many whole numbers can you multiply by 7 and still get less than 50?

7 of them (1 through 7, since 7 × 7 = 49)

3. Can more than one pair of whole numbers multiply to make 13?

No - 13 is prime, so only 1 × 13

A factor of a number divides it evenly, with no remainder left over.

1.2.3Prime vs. Composite

  • A prime number has exactly 2 factors: 1 and itself.
  • A composite number has more than 2 factors.
  • The number 1 is neither prime nor composite. Its only factor is itself.
EX 4. List the first six prime numbers, then six composite numbers.

Prime: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13

Composite: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12

1.2.4Prime Factorization & Factor Trees

Every number can be written as a product of prime numbers. A factor tree is how we find them.
  1. Start with the number.
  2. Split it into any two numbers that multiply to give it.
  3. Keep splitting until every branch ends in a prime number (these ends are called leaves).
  4. The prime factorization is the product of the leaves.
EX 5. Build a factor tree for 360 starting with 360 = 18 × 20.

Prime factorization: 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5

Written with exponents: 23 × 32 × 5

18 = 2 × 3 × 3    20 = 2 × 2 × 5

EX 6. Now build a different tree for 360, starting with 360 = 3 × 120. What do you notice about the answer?

Prime factorization: 23 × 32 × 5

The same prime factorization. The starting split does not change it.

Gotcha: which factors should I start with?
There is no single right answer. Starting with the smallest factors makes a taller, skinnier tree; starting with a pair close together makes a shorter, wider tree. Either way the prime factorization is the same either way.
EX 7. Negative numbers: factor out −1 first. Build a tree for −24.

Prime factorization of −24: −1 × 23 × 3

Challenge

6. Can two different numbers have the same prime factorization? Why or why not?

No. Every number has exactly one prime factorization, so the same set of primes always multiplies back to the same single number.
