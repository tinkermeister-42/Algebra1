Teacher Key - answers in red
Objectives
  • Find the factors and multiples of integers
  • Tell if a number is prime or composite
  • Break numbers into prime factors using a factor tree

Warm-Up

1. How many different pairs of whole numbers multiply to give 36?

5 pairs: 1×36, 2×18, 3×12, 4×9, 6×6

2. How many whole numbers can you multiply by 7 and still get less than 50?

7 of them (1 through 7, since 7 × 7 = 49)

3. Can more than one pair of whole numbers multiply to make 13?

No. 13 is prime, so only 1 × 13.

1.2.1What Are Factors?

A factor of a number is a whole number that divides it evenly (with no remainder).
EX 1. Find every factor of 12 by listing the factor pairs.

1 × 12 = 12

2 × 6 = 12

3 × 4 = 12

Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12

EX 2. Can a number have just one factor? Which one?your turn

Yes, only 1

1.2.2What Are Multiples?

A multiple is what you get when you multiply a number by a whole number. The multiples of 5 are 5 × 1, 5 × 2, 5 × 3, and so on.
EX 3. List the first six multiples of 5.

5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, …

EX 4. Mark the multiples of 3 on this number line. Label your scale.

0, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, …

1.2.3Prime vs. Composite

A prime number has only 2 factors: 1 and itself.  Examples: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13…
A composite number has more than 2 factors.  Examples: 4, 6, 8, 9, 10…
The number 1 is neither prime nor composite.

1.2.4Prime Factorization & Factor Trees

Every number can be broken into a product of prime numbers. A factor tree finds these prime factors. The ends of the branches are called leaves.
EX 5. Build a factor tree for 360. Start with 360 = 18 × 20.

18 = 3 × 6 = 3 × 2 × 3    20 = 4 × 5 = 2 × 2 × 5

Prime factorization: 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5

With exponents: 23 × 32 × 5

EX 6. Build a different tree for 360. Start with 360 = 3 × 120.

120 = 2 × 60,  60 = 15 × 4,  15 = 5 × 3,  4 = 2 × 2

Prime factorization: 23 × 32 × 5 (the same)

EX 7. Negative numbers: factor out −1 first. Build a tree for −24.

Prime factorization of −24: −1 × 23 × 3

EX 8. Find at least one more factor tree for 360.your turn

Answers vary. For example, 360 = 10 × 36. Every tree ends in 23 × 32 × 5.

Challenge

6. Can two different numbers have the same prime factorization? Why or why not?

No. Every number has exactly one prime factorization, so the same set of primes always multiplies back to the same single number.
