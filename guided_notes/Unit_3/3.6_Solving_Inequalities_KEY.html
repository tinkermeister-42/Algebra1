3.6  Solving Inequalities

Guided Notes - Unit 3: Solving Linear Equations - DHS Algebra 1
Teacher Key: answers in red
Objectives
  • Represent and graph solutions to inequalities on a number line
  • Solve inequalities using addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division
  • Use inequalities to model and solve real-world problems

Warm-Up

1. Without solving, which is larger: 4x + 9 or 6x + 3?

It depends. They are equal at x = 3; below that 4x + 9 is larger, above it 6x + 3 is.

2. Simplify 3(2x − 5) − (x + 7)

6x − 15 − x − 7 = 5x − 22

3. You have $50. After a $12 game, how many $8 accessories could you afford if you spent it all?

50 − 12 = 38, and 38 ÷ 8 = 4.75, so 4 accessories.

Why we need inequalities
Bread costs $3.50 and candy is $1.25 a package. With $20, an equation gives c = 13.2 packages, but you cannot buy part of a package. Equations give one exact answer; real problems often have a range of answers.

3.6.4Real-World Problems

EX 11. Back to the candy. You have $20, bread costs $3.50, and candy is $1.25 per package. How many packages can you afford?

Let c be the number of packages you purchase.

Inequality: 3.50 + 1.25c ≤ 20

Solution: c ≤ 13.2    So you can buy 13 packages.

Why did we round down instead of up? You can only buy whole packages. 14 packages cost $21, more than $20.

EX 12. Saving for shoes. You have $40 saved and earn $9 per hour. You want at least $100. How many whole hours must you work?

Let h be the number of hours you work.

Inequality: 40 + 9h ≥ 100

Solution: h ≥ 6.6 (about 6.67)    So you must work 7 hours.

Why round up this time? after 6 hours you only have $94
