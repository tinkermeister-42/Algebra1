3.4  Equations With Variables on Both Sides

Guided Notes - Unit 3: Solving Linear Equations - DHS Algebra 1
Teacher Key - answers in red
Objectives
  • Solve equations with variables on both sides using inverse operations
  • Simplify with distribution or like terms before solving

Warm-Up

1. Solve 5x − 2 = 13

5x = 15, x = 3

2. Are 3(5x − 1) and 12x + 3(x − 1) equivalent?

Yes - both simplify to 15x − 3.

3. Swanhilda solved 3(x − 7) = 3 like this. Did she do it correctly?

3x − 21 = 3  →  3x − 21 + 21 = 3  →  3x = 3  →  x = 1

No - she added 21 to the left only. It should be 3x = 24, so x = 8.

3.4.1The Basic Strategy

One new step: get all the variable terms on one side and all the constants on the other.
EX 1. Three apples and 1 weight balance one apple and 7 weights. Find a.
StepWhat I did to both sides
3a + 1 = a + 7the equation
2a + 1 = 7subtract a from both sides
2a = 6subtract 1 from both sides
a = 3divide both sides by 2
Gotcha - do it to both sides
Taking an apple off only the right pan breaks the balance. Whatever you do to one side you must do to the other.
EX 2. Solve 4x + 5 = 2x + 9.
4x + 5 = 2x + 9the equation
2x + 5 = 9subtract 2x from both sides
2x = 4subtract 5 from both sides
x = 2divide both sides by 2
EX 3. Solve 5x + 3 = 2x + 12.

3x + 3 = 12  →  3x = 9  →  x = 3

3.4.2Simplifying Before Solving

Remember - the order of attack
  1. Simplify each side (distribute and combine like terms).
  2. Move the variable terms to one side.
  3. Move the constants to the other side.
  4. Solve for the variable.
  5. Check your solution.
EX 4. Solve 2(x + 3) = 3x + 1.
2(x + 3) = 3x + 1the equation
2x + 6 = 3x + 1distribute
6 = x + 1subtract 2x from both sides
5 = xsubtract 1 from both sides
x = 5check: 2(8) = 16 and 3(5) + 1 = 16 ✓
EX 5. Solve 4x + 6 + x = 2x + 12.
4x + 6 + x = 2x + 12 the equation
5x + 6 = 2x + 12combine like terms
3x = 6subtract 2x and 6 from both sides
x = 2divide both sides by 3
EX 6. Solve 3(x − 2) + 4 = 2(x + 1).
3(x − 2) + 4 = 2(x + 1) the equation
3x − 6 + 4 = 2x + 2distribute both sides
3x − 2 = 2x + 2combine like terms
x − 2 = 2subtract 2x from both sides
x = 4add 2 to both sides
EX 7. Solve 2(x + 4) = x + 10.

2x + 8 = x + 10  →  x = 2

3.4.3Word Problems

When two different plans describe the same total, you get a variable on both sides. Write each side, set them equal, and solve.
EX 8. Two pay plans. Alex earns a $6 bonus plus $15/hour. Blair earns an $18 bonus plus $12/hour. When do they earn the same?

Let h = the number of hours worked    Equation: 6 + 15h = 18 + 12h

Answer: after 4 hours

3h = 12, so h = 4

EX 9. Equal perimeters. Rectangle A is (x + 6) by (x + 2). Rectangle B is (2x − 1) by (x + 3). They have the same perimeter. Find x.

Perimeter formula: P = 2l + 2w

Equation: 4x + 16 = 6x + 4

A: 2(x+6) + 2(x+2) = 4x + 16    B: 2(2x−1) + 2(x+3) = 6x + 4

12 = 2x, so x = 6

EX 10. Two vendors. Vendor A: $18 per sweatshirt, 2 free, plus $12 shipping. Vendor B: $15 each plus $42 flat shipping. When do the totals match?

Vendor A total: 18(s − 2) + 12    Vendor B total: 15s + 42

Answer: 22 sweatshirts

18s − 24 = 15s + 42  →  3s = 66

EX 11. Break-even. Plan A: $8 per month plus $3 per movie. Plan B: $2 per month plus $5 per movie. When do they cost the same?

8 + 3m = 2 + 5m  →  6 = 2m

m = 3 movies

Word Problems

21. Plan A charges $10 per month plus $4 per movie. Plan B charges $6 per month plus $5 per movie. After how many movies is the monthly cost the same?

Equation: 10 + 4m = 6 + 5m

m = 4 movies

22. Service A charges $25 per visit plus $15 per hour. Service B charges $40 per visit plus $10 per hour. After how many hours are the costs equal?

Equation: 25 + 15h = 40 + 10h

5h = 15, so 3 hours

23. Two sides of a trapezoid are equal. One is 4x + 3 cm and the other is 2(x + 6) cm. Find x.

4x + 3 = 2(x + 6) = 2x + 12

2x = 9, so x = 4.5

1200 + 35s = 50s − 300

1500 = 15s, so s = 100 square meters
