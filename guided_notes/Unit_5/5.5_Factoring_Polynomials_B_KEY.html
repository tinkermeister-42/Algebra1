Teacher Key - answers in red
Objectives
  • Factor trinomials when a ≠ 1 using structured strategies
  • Recognize and factor difference of squares and perfect-square trinomials
  • Decide when a trinomial is not factorable over the integers

Warm-Up

1. When you factored x2 + 9x + 20, what made the process predictable?

a = 1, so both factors start with x. You only had to find factors of c that add to b.

2. Look at 6x2 + 11x + 3. What is different about its structure?

The leading coefficient is not 1, so the first terms of the factors are not both x.

3. Swanhilda says 6x2 + 7x + 10 = (x + 2)(x + 5). Why can't that be right?

(x+2)(x+5) = x2 + 7x + 10 - the leading coefficient is 1, not 6.

5.5.1Factoring When a ≠ 1

Before, every trinomial started with x2, so the first terms of the factors were x automatically. With ax2 + bx + c they are not.
The one change: instead of factors of c that add to b, look for factors of a · c that add to b.
Then fill in the outside of the model by taking the GCF of each row and each column.

a · c = 18

EX 4. Factor 14w2 + 3w − 2.

a·c = −28; 7 and −4 add to 3.

EX 8. Factor 21r2 + 30r + 9 completely.

GCF = 3:  3(7r2 + 10r + 3)

7 and 3 add to 10, so 7r2 + 7r + 3r + 3 = (7r + 3)(r + 1)

= 3(7r + 3)(r + 1)

5.5.2Special Patterns

Difference of squares: there is no middle term, because the two middle boxes cancel.

(px)2 − q2 = (px − q)(px + q)

EX 9. Factor 16x2 − 9     EX 10. Factor 49p2 − 25

16x2 − 9 = (4x − 3)(4x + 3)

49p2 − 25 = (7p − 5)(7p + 5)

Perfect-square trinomial: the middle term is exactly twice the product of the two roots, 2pq.

(px)2 ± 2pqx + q2 = (px ± q)2

EX 11. Factor 25m2 + 30m + 9.

Rewrite it in the pattern: (5m)2 + (2 · 5 · 3)m + 32

= (5m + 3)2

EX 12. Factor 36z2 − 12z + 1     EX 13. Factor 9a2 − 30a + 25

36z2 − 12z + 1 = (6z − 1)2

9a2 − 30a + 25 = (3a − 5)2

Prime trinomials
Some polynomials cannot be factored over the integers. Take 3x2 + 10x + 5.
Factors of 15Sum
1 × 1516
3 × 58

No pair adds to 10, so this polynomial is prime.
