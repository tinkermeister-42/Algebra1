5.4.1Factoring Out a GCF

Factoring is the reverse of multiplying. Instead of expanding, we rewrite the expression as a product.
The GCF is the largest number or expression that divides every term.
EX 1. Factor 12x + 18.

12x breaks into 2 · 2 · 3 · x    18 breaks into 2 · 3 · 3

GCF = 6

12x + 18 = 6(2x + 3)

EX 2. Factor 12x3 − 3x2. The GCF has a variable part too.

12x3 = 3 · 4 · x2 · x    3x2 = 3 · x2

GCF = 3x2    Factored: 3x2(4x − 1)

EX 3. Factor 15y3 − 5y.

5y(3y2 − 1)

EX 4. Factor −x2 − 4x − 3. The leading term is negative.

When the first term is negative it is easiest to take out −1 first, which keeps the inside positive.

Factored: −(x2 + 4x + 3) = −(x + 1)(x + 3)

EX 5. Factor −18xy2 + 27x2y.

9xy(3x − 2y)

5.4.2Area Models and Basic Trinomials

EX 6. First look forward: multiply (x + 3)(x + 2) and watch where the middle term comes from.
x2
xx22x
33x6

Product: x2 + 5x + 6

The top-left box comes from x · x.

The bottom-right box comes from 3 · 2.

The two diagonal boxes combine to make the middle x term.

To factor, run that backwards. Put x2 in the top-left and the constant in the bottom-right, then find two numbers whose product is the constant c and whose sum is the middle coefficient b.

5.4.3Mixed Expressions: GCF and Area Model

If every term shares a factor, take out the GCF first, then use the area model on what is left.
Gotcha: put it back!
If you factor out a GCF, do not forget to write the GCF back in your final answer.
EX 14. Factor 4x2 + 20x + 24 completely.

GCF = 4  →  4(x2 + 5x + 6)

x3
xx23x
22x6

Final answer: 4(x + 2)(x + 3)

EX 15. Factor −15x2 + 45x − 30 completely.

GCF = −15  →  −15(x2 − 3x + 2)

x−1
xx2−x
−2−2x2

Final answer: −15(x − 1)(x − 2)

EX 16. Factor −45x2 − 5x3 − 70x completely. Put it in standard form first!

Standard form: −5x3 − 45x2 − 70x

GCF = −5x  →  −5x(x2 + 9x + 14)

x7
xx27x
22x14

Final answer: −5x(x + 2)(x + 7)

EX 17. Factor −9b3 + 9b2 + 18b completely.

GCF = −9b:  −9b(b2 − b − 2)

= −9b(b − 2)(b + 1)

Remember: the order of attack
  1. Put the polynomial in standard form.
  2. Factor out a GCF if there is one.
  3. If the first term is negative, include a negative sign with the GCF.
  4. Use an area model on what remains.
  5. Check your answer by multiplying it back out.
