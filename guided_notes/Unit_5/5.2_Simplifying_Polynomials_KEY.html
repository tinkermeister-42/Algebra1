5.2.1Understanding Polynomials

A polynomial is a sum or difference of terms. Each term has a coefficient (a number), a variable, and a whole number exponent. The constant 5 fits too, because 5 = 5x0.
Polynomials:  3x2 + 2x − 5,   7y3 − 4y,   9
Not polynomials:  2x and x−3 + 4 (negative exponent),  √x + 5 (fractional exponent)
1 term: monomial (7x3).   2 terms: binomial (4x + 1).   3 terms: trinomial (x2 + 3x + 2).   More than 3 terms: no special name.
The degree of a polynomial is the largest exponent in it.

5.2.2Simplifying Polynomials

The process

1. Distribute if parentheses are present.

3. Combine like terms.

2. Apply exponent rules when needed.

4. Rewrite in standard form.

3x · 2x2 + 3x · 5x − 3x · 4 = 6x3 + 15x2 − 12x

6x2 − 3x + 15 − x2 − 4x + 1

= (6x2 − x2) + (−3x − 4x) + (15 + 1)

= 5x2 − 7x + 16

Summary
binomialhighestlowestnegativetermstrinomial26

1. A polynomial with 3 terms is a trinomial.

2. The degree of 5z6 − 2z2 − 3 is 6.

3. x−3 + 4 is not a polynomial. It has a negative exponent.

4. In standard form, the terms go from the highest degree to the lowest.

5. In 3x2 + 5x2 − 4x, 3x2 and 5x2 are like terms.

Challenge

26. Add (x + a) + (x + b) + (x + c). After combining like terms the result has no x term. What must be true about a, b, and c?

The sum is 3x + (a + b + c).

The x-coefficient is 3 no matter what a, b, c are, so the x term can never disappear. The condition is impossible.

(If instead the constant term is to vanish, then a + b + c = 0.)
