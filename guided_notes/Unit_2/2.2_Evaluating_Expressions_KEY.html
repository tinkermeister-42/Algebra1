Warm-Up

1. Simplify 32 + 2 · 4 − 1

9 + 8 − 1 = 16

2. How are 2x + 5 and 5 + 2x different? How are they the same?

Same value for every x - addition can be done in either order.

3. A student evaluates (−3)2 and gets −9. Correct? Why?

No - (−3)2 = 9. The parentheses square the negative too. −32 is the one that equals −9.

2.2.1What Does It Mean to Evaluate?

To evaluate an expression means to find its value by substituting a number for the variable and then simplifying.
When you see =, think “is”.
EX 1. The Great Cookie Caper. Match each part of the story to the math.
StoryMath
A thief stole the cookies.an expression with a variable in it
“The thief” - we do not know who.x - the unknown
A witness says the thief is Bob.x = Bob - substitution
Bob stole the cookies.the evaluated expression
EX 2. Evaluate 4x + 1 when x = 3.

Substitute: 4( 3 ) + 1

Simplify: 4(3) + 1 = 13

Gotcha - negatives
Before you substitute, put parentheses where the variable was. That keeps the negative signs from getting lost.
EX 3. Evaluate 3 − 2x when x = −4.

3 − 2(−4) = 3 + 8 = 11

EX 4. Evaluate 5x − 2 when x = −3.

5(−3) − 2 = −15 − 2 = −17

2.2.2Using PEMDAS with Substitution

Evaluating is just one extra step on top of the order of operations: first substitute, then apply PEMDAS.
EX 5. Evaluate 2(x + 3)2 when x = 1.

2(1 + 3)2 = 2(4)2 = 2(16) = 32

EX 6. The variable appears twice. Evaluate 3(2 − y) + 5(y − 2) when y = −4.

3(2 − (−4)) + 5((−4) − 2)

= 3(6) + 5(−6) = 18 − 30 = −12

EX 7. Evaluate 20(x − 2) + 2x2 when x = 5.

20(5 − 2) + 2(5)2 = 20(3) + 2(25) = 60 + 50 = 110

2.2.3Expressions With More Than One Variable

Nothing changes - substitute for every variable, then simplify.
EX 8. Evaluate 2a − 3b when a = 2 and b = 1.

2(2) − 3(1) = 4 − 3 = 1

EX 9. Evaluate 12x − 3y2 when x = 7 and y = −3. Careful with the negative.

12(7) − 3(−3)2 = 84 − 3(9) = 84 − 27 = 57

EX 10. Evaluate 12x2 − 3y when x = 4 and y = 3.

12(4)2 − 3(3) = 8 − 9 = −1

2.2.4Testing for Equivalence

Two expressions are equivalent if they always give the same result. Substitute the same number into both and compare.
EX 11. Are 5x − 25 and 5(x − 5) equivalent? Test x = 9.

5(9) − 25 = 45 − 25 = 20

5(9 − 5) = 5(4) = 20

Conclusion: both give 20 - equivalent (and 5(x−5) expands to 5x−25)

EX 12. Are 3(x − 1) and 3x − 2 equivalent? Test x = 2.

3(2 − 1) = 3(1) = 3

3(2) − 2 = 6 − 2 = 4

Conclusion: 3 ≠ 4, so they are not equivalent

Gotcha - what does a test actually prove?
Different results → the expressions are not equivalent.
Same result → this is only evidence that they might be equivalent. It does not prove it.
Try 6y and 2y at y = 0: both give 0    Now try y = 3: 18 and 6 - different, so not equivalent
EX 13. Test whether 9y − 3 and 3(3y − 2) + 3 are equivalent using y = 2. What does your answer tell you?

9(2) − 3 = 15

3(3(2) − 2) + 3 = 3(4) + 3 = 15

This tells me: they match here, and expanding gives 9y − 6 + 3 = 9y − 3 - so they really are equivalent

Equivalent - the 2a2 and 4ab terms cancel, leaving 2b2.
