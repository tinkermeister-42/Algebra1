4.5.1Solving Systems by Graphing

A system of equations is two or more equations that share the same variables. The solution is a point that makes every equation true. On a graph it is the point where the lines cross.
EX 1. Graph and solve.
y = 2x + 1 y = −x + 4

Solution: ( 1 , 3 )

Check it in both equations:

2(1) + 1 = 3 ✓    −1 + 4 = 3 ✓

EX 2. Graph and solve.
y = 12x + 3 y = −x + 6

Solution: ( 2 , 4 )

EX 3. Gym memberships. Plan A is $10 to sign up plus $5 per class. Plan B is $25 plus $2 per class.

System:

y = 5x + 10 y = 2x + 25

They meet at ( 5 , 35 ).

Meaning: after 5 classes both plans cost $35. Before that, Plan A is cheaper.

EX 4. Plumber vs. electrician. A plumber charges $40 plus $60 per hour. An electrician charges $100 plus $50 per hour. The lines meet at (6, 400).

What does the x-value tell you? after 6 hours the two charge the same

What does the y-value tell you? that shared price is $400

EX 5. Phone plans. Graph and interpret.
y = 15x + 40 y = 25x + 10
 (y = total cost, x = months)

Solution: ( 3 , 85 )

Interpretation:

After 3 months both plans have cost $85. Before that the $25/month plan is cheaper; after it, the $15/month plan is.

4.5.2Solving by Substitution

Steps
  1. Solve one equation for one variable.
  2. Substitute that expression into the other equation.
  3. Solve for the remaining variable.
  4. Plug back in to find the other one.
Why is this allowed? Because if two expressions are equal, you can replace one with the other.
EX 6. Solve by substitution.
y = x + 2 2x + y = 8

Solution: ( 2 , 4 )

2x + (x + 2) = 8  →  3x = 6

EX 7. Solve by substitution.
y = 3x − 8 x + y = −24

Solution: ( −4 , −20 )

x + (3x − 8) = −24  →  4x = −16

4.5.3Solving by Elimination

Steps
  1. Line up both equations.
  2. Add or subtract so one variable cancels.
  3. Solve for the remaining variable.
  4. Substitute back to find the other.
EX 8. Solve by elimination.
2x + y = 10 2x − y = 2

Which variable cancels, and do you add or subtract? y cancels, so add the two equations

Solution: ( 3 , 4 )

EX 9. Solve by elimination.
x + y = 9 x − y = 3

Adding gives 2x = 12, so x = 6 and y = 3 → (6, 3)

When nothing cancels
Sometimes you must multiply one or both equations first so that the coefficients match.
EX 10. Solve by elimination.
3x + 2y = 16 2x + 3y = 14

Multiply the first by 3 and the second by 2:

Solution: ( 4 , 2 )

9x + 6y = 48 and 4x + 6y = 28; subtracting gives 5x = 20.

21. The system
y = mx + 4 y = −2x + b
intersects at (2, 0). Find m and b.

At (2, 0): 0 = 2m + 4 → m = −2, and 0 = −4 + b → b = 4.

Note both equations then read y = −2x + 4 - the lines coincide, so they meet everywhere, not just at (2, 0).
