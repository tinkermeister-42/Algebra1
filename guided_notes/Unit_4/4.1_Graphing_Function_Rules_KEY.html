Cross two number lines. That is the coordinate plane.

4.1.1The Coordinate Plane

The coordinate plane is two number lines crossed together.
  • The x-axis runs horizontally, left and right.
  • The y-axis runs vertically, up and down.
  • They meet at the origin, the point ( 0 , 0 ).
  • A point's ordered pair is ( x , y ): first move right or left, then move up or down.
  • The axes make 4 quadrants, numbered counterclockwise starting in the top right.

4.1.2Function Rules and Tables of Values

Each input/output pair is a point you can plot. Connect enough of them and you can see what the function looks like.
f(x) means the same thing as y. So f(x) = 2x + 1 could also be written y = 2x + 1.

Points (−3,6) … (2,−4). Same steepness as y = 2x but falling instead of rising, a mirror image across the x-axis.

11. Graph y = −x + 2 for x = −2, 0, 2. What happens to the y-values as x increases?

(−2,4), (0,2), (2,0). As x goes up by 1, y goes down by 1.

12. f(x) = 2x + 4 and g(x) = 2x − 2 are graphed together. How are the lines alike? How are they different?

Alike:

Both have slope 2, so they are parallel and never cross.

Different:

Different y-intercepts - 4 and −2, so g sits 6 units below f.

13. Graph y = 3 and y = −2 on the same grid. What kind of lines are they?

Both are horizontal lines. Every point has the same y.
