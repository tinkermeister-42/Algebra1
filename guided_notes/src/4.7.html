An inequality's solution is not a single line. It is a whole {{a}}region{{/a}} of the plane.

4.7.3Systems of Linear Inequalities

With two or more inequalities, each one shades its own region. The solution set is where the regions {{a}}overlap{{/a}}. Every point there makes {{a}}every{{/a}} inequality true.
EX 7. Graph the system.
y ≤ 2x + 3 y > −x + 1

First line: {{a}}solid{{/a}}, shade {{a}}below{{/a}}

Second line: {{a}}dashed{{/a}}, shade {{a}}above{{/a}}

Test (4, 2) in both: {{a}}2 ≤ 11 ✓ and 2 > −3 ✓ - yes, it is a solution{{/a}}

{{grid}}
EX 8. Graph the larger system.
y ≤ −x + 5 y > x + 1 y ≥ 0

Describe the solution region:

{{a}}

Above the dashed y = x + 1, on or above the x-axis, and on or below y = −x + 5. The boundaries meet at (2, 3); the region opens out to the left, so it is unbounded.

{{/a}}

EX 9. Graph and describe the solution region.
y > −x + 2 y < 3x + 6
{{a}}

A wedge opening to the right from the vertex (−1, 3): above the dashed y = −x + 2 and below the dashed y = 3x + 6.
