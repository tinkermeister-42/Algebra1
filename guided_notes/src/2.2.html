2.2.1What Does It Mean to Evaluate?

To evaluate an expression means to find its value by {{a}}substituting{{/a}} a number for the variable and then {{a}}simplifying{{/a}}.
When you see =, think “{{a}}is{{/a}}”.
EX 1. The Great Cookie Caper. Match each part of the story to the math.
StoryMath
A thief stole the cookies.{{a}}an expression with a variable in it{{/a}}
“The thief” — we do not know who.{{a}}x — the unknown{{/a}}
A witness says the thief is Bob.{{a}}x = Bob — substitution{{/a}}
Bob stole the cookies.{{a}}the evaluated expression{{/a}}
EX 2. Evaluate 4x + 1 when x = 3.

Substitute: 4( {{a}}3{{/a}} ) + 1

Simplify: {{a}}4(3) + 1 = 13{{/a}}

Gotcha — negatives
Before you substitute, put {{a}}parentheses{{/a}} where the variable was. That keeps the negative signs from getting lost.
EX 3. Evaluate 3 − 2x when x = −4.
{{a}}

3 − 2(−4) = 3 + 8 = 11

{{/a}}
EX 4. Evaluate 5x − 2 when x = −3.
{{a}}

5(−3) − 2 = −15 − 2 = −17

{{/a}}

2.2.2Using PEMDAS with Substitution

Evaluating is just one extra step on top of the order of operations: first {{a}}substitute{{/a}}, then apply {{a}}PEMDAS{{/a}}.
EX 5. Evaluate 2(x + 3)2 when x = 1.
{{a}}

2(1 + 3)2 = 2(4)2 = 2(16) = 32

{{/a}}
EX 6. The variable appears twice. Evaluate 3(2 − y) + 5(y − 2) when y = −4.
{{a}}

3(2 − (−4)) + 5((−4) − 2)

= 3(6) + 5(−6) = 18 − 30 = −12

{{/a}}
EX 7. Evaluate 20(x − 2) + 2x2 when x = 5.
{{a}}

20(5 − 2) + 2(5)2 = 20(3) + 2(25) = 60 + 50 = 110

{{/a}}

2.2.3Expressions With More Than One Variable

Nothing changes — substitute for {{a}}every{{/a}} variable, then simplify.
EX 8. Evaluate 2a − 3b when a = 2 and b = 1.
{{a}}

2(2) − 3(1) = 4 − 3 = 1

{{/a}}
EX 9. Evaluate 12x − 3y2 when x = 7 and y = −3. Careful with the negative.
{{a}}

12(7) − 3(−3)2 = 84 − 3(9) = 84 − 27 = 57

{{/a}}
EX 10. Evaluate {{f 1/2}}x2 − 3y when x = 4 and y = 3.
{{a}}

{{f 1/2}}(4)2 − 3(3) = 8 − 9 = −1

{{/a}}

2.2.4Testing for Equivalence

Two expressions are equivalent if they always give the {{a}}same{{/a}} result. Substitute the same number into both and compare.
EX 11. Are 5x − 25 and 5(x − 5) equivalent? Test x = 9.
{{a}}

5(9) − 25 = 45 − 25 = 20

{{/a}}
{{a}}

5(9 − 5) = 5(4) = 20

{{/a}}

Conclusion: {{a}}both give 20 — equivalent (and 5(x−5) expands to 5x−25){{/a}}

EX 12. Are 3(x − 1) and 3x − 2 equivalent? Test x = 2.
{{a}}

3(2 − 1) = 3(1) = 3

{{/a}}
{{a}}

3(2) − 2 = 6 − 2 = 4

{{/a}}

Conclusion: {{a}}3 ≠ 4, so they are not equivalent{{/a}}

Gotcha — what does a test actually prove?
Different results → the expressions are {{a}}not equivalent{{/a}}.
Same result → this is only {{a}}evidence{{/a}} that they might be equivalent. It does not prove it.
Try 6y and 2y at y = 0: {{a}}both give 0{{/a}}    Now try y = 3: {{a}}18 and 6 — different, so not equivalent{{/a}}
EX 13. Test whether 9y − 3 and 3(3y − 2) + 3 are equivalent using y = 2. What does your answer tell you?
{{a}}

9(2) − 3 = 15

{{/a}}
{{a}}

3(3(2) − 2) + 3 = 3(4) + 3 = 15

{{/a}}

This tells me: {{a}}they match here, and expanding gives 9y − 6 + 3 = 9y − 3 — so they really are equivalent{{/a}}
