Gotcha: top and bottom
{{f 4/4}} = 1, so multiplying by it does not change the value. Multiply only the bottom and you have changed the fraction into a different number.
Different denominators mean the pieces are {{a}}different sizes{{/a}}. A common denominator is a number both denominators {{a}}divide into evenly{{/a}}.
To find one: list the {{a}}multiples{{/a}} of each and take the first number both lists show.

Multiples of 3: {{a}}3, 6, 9, 12, 15…{{/a}}

Multiples of 4: {{a}}4, 8, 12, 16…{{/a}}  →  common denominator: {{a}}12{{/a}}

Then {{a}}divide{{/a}} to find what each fraction gets multiplied by: 12 ÷ 3 = {{a}}4{{/a}} and 12 ÷ 4 = {{a}}3{{/a}}.

1.5.3Adding & Subtracting Mixed Numbers

Why not combine the whole numbers and then the fractions? It breaks on 6{{f 1/4}} − 3{{f 3/8}}, because {{f 1/4}} is {{a}}smaller than{{/a}} {{f 3/8}} and you have to {{a}}borrow{{/a}}. Turn them into fractions first instead.
