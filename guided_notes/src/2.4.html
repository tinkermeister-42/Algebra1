2.4.1What Is the Distributive Property?

To distribute is to hand the multiplier to {{a}}every{{/a}} term inside the parentheses.

a(b + c) = {{a}}ab + ac{{/a}}   and   a(b − c) = {{a}}ab − ac{{/a}}

EX 1. Expand 3(x + 2). Draw the arrows from the 3 to each term.

3( x   +   2 )  =  {{a}}3x + 6{{/a}}

With algebra tiles: 3 groups of (x + 2) gives {{a}}3{{/a}} x-tiles and {{a}}6{{/a}} unit tiles.

Gotcha — do not skip a term
Multiply every term inside.   4(x + 2y + 5) = {{a}}4x + 8y + 20{{/a}}

2.4.4Reverse It: Factoring Out a Common Factor

If ab + ac = a(b + c), then we can factor out the {{a}}common factor (the GCF){{/a}}. This is the distributive property {{a}}in reverse{{/a}}.

23. Simplify 4(2x + 3) − (2x + 3) two ways: distribute first, then factor out the common (2x + 3) first. Why do both give the same result?

Distribute first:{{a}}   8x + 12 − 2x − 3 = 6x + 9{{/a}}

Factor first:{{a}}   (2x + 3)(4 − 1) = 3(2x + 3) = 6x + 9{{/a}}

{{a}}

Both work because factoring is just the distributive property read backwards.
