2.3.1What Are Like Terms?

Two terms are like terms when their {{a}}variable{{/a}} part, the letters and the exponents, matches {{a}}exactly{{/a}}.
The sign (+ or −) belongs {{a}}to{{/a}} the term. In 7x − 2x the terms are {{a}}7x{{/a}} and {{a}}−2x{{/a}}.

p3 and p2 are {{a}}not{{/a}} like terms. Same letter, different {{a}}exponent{{/a}}.

2.3.2How to Combine Like Terms

Three steps, like a cashier counting a mixed drawer:

1. Sort: {{a}}put like terms together{{/a}}

2. Group: {{a}}use parentheses around each group{{/a}}

3. Add: {{a}}add the coefficients{{/a}}

EX 3. Simplify 9a + 4c + 8b − 3a + b − 2c + 2a − 6b − 4a
Sort by type{{a}}a: 9a, −3a, 2a, −4a   b: 8b, b, −6b   c: 4c, −2c{{/a}}
Group like terms{{a}}(9a − 3a + 2a − 4a) + (8b + b − 6b) + (4c − 2c){{/a}}
Add each group{{a}}4a + 3b + 2c{{/a}}
Why are we allowed to reorder and regroup?
We can change the order because addition is {{a}}commutative{{/a}}. We can regroup because addition is {{a}}associative{{/a}}.
EX 4. Simplify −1.5x + 2.5 + 3.5x2 − 0.5 + 5.5x − 2 − 4.0x − 4.5x2
Sort{{a}}x2: 3.5x2, −4.5x2   x: −1.5x, 5.5x, −4.0x   constants: 2.5, −0.5, −2{{/a}}
Group{{a}}(3.5x2 − 4.5x2) + (−1.5x + 5.5x − 4.0x) + (2.5 − 0.5 − 2){{/a}}
Add{{a}}−x2   (the x terms and the constants both cancel){{/a}}
Gotcha: watch those negative signs
When you group, the negative stays {{a}}with its own term{{/a}}. If you write −(1.5x + 5.5x − 4.0x) instead, you have made the {{a}}whole{{/a}} group negative and the answer changes.

2.3.3Checking Your Work

Unsure? {{a}}Substitute{{/a}} a number into the original expression and into your simplified one. If the results differ, you made a {{a}}mistake{{/a}}.
EX 6. Simplify 5x − 3x2 + 2x − 7 + 3x2 + 3, then check with x = 2.

Simplified: {{a}}7x − 4{{/a}}

Check the original at x = 2:{{a}}   10 − 12 + 4 − 7 + 12 + 3 = 10{{/a}}

Check the simplified at x = 2:{{a}}   7(2) − 4 = 10 ✓{{/a}}

Challenge

23. Build an expression equivalent to 7x − 4 that uses at least four terms. Explain how you know it is equivalent.
{{a}}

Answers vary. For example 3x + 2 + 4x − 6.

Combining like terms gives 7x − 4, so it is equivalent for every value of x.

{{/a}}
24. Give two expressions that are not equivalent but give the same value at x = 0. Why doesn't that single test prove equivalence?
{{a}}

For example 3x and 5x. Both are 0 at x = 0.

One test only shows they agree at that single value; equivalence means agreeing at every value.
