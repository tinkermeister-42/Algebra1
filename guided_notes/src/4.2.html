4.2.1What Is Slope?

Picture climbing a stairway. Each step moves you {{a}}forward{{/a}} and also {{a}}up or down{{/a}}. Comparing those two amounts tells you how {{a}}steep{{/a}} the stairs are. That is slope.

m = {{f rise/run}} = {{f Δy/Δx}}

Δ (delta) means {{a}}change in{{/a}}. So Δy is {{a}}the change in y (the rise){{/a}} and Δx is {{a}}the change in x (the run){{/a}}.

When counting boxes is impractical, use the formula. For (x1, y1) and (x2, y2):

m = {{f Δy/Δx}} = {{a}}{{f y2 − y1/x2 − x1}}{{/a}}

Challenge

24. Two lines have slopes m1 = 3 and m2 = −{{f 1/3}}. What is true about how these lines relate to each other?
{{a}}

3 and −{{f 1/3}} are negative reciprocals. Their product is −1, so the lines are perpendicular.

{{/a}}
25. A line passes through the origin and a point (x, y). Show that if the slope doubles while x stays fixed, the new point satisfies y′ = 2y. What does that say about steepness?
{{a}}

Through the origin the slope is m = {{f y/x}}.

Doubling it: {{f y′/x}} = 2 · {{f y/x}}, and multiplying both sides by x gives y′ = 2y.

So at the same run the line rises twice as far. That is twice as steep.

{{/a}}
