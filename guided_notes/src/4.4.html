Steps
  1. Find where the line crosses the y-axis - that is {{a}}b{{/a}}.
  2. Pick two clear points and count rise over run to get {{a}}m{{/a}}.
  3. Substitute both into {{a}}y = mx + b{{/a}}.

4.4.3Slope and a Point Are Known

Steps
  1. Start with y = mx + b.
  2. Substitute the slope and the {{a}}x and y{{/a}} of the point.
  3. Solve for {{a}}b{{/a}}.
  4. Write the final equation.
EX 4. Slope m = 3 through (2, 5).

Substitute: {{a}}5 = 3(2) + b{{/a}}

b = {{a}}−1{{/a}}    Equation: {{a}}y = 3x − 1{{/a}}

EX 5. Slope m = −2 through (1, 4).
{{a}}

4 = −2(1) + b  →  b = 6

y = −2x + 6

{{/a}}
EX 6. Slope m = {{f 1/2}} through (4, 3).
{{a}}

3 = {{f 1/2}}(4) + b = 2 + b  →  b = 1

y = {{f 1/2}}x + 1

{{/a}}
Another way - point-slope form

y − y1 = m(x − x1)

Redo EX 5 with point-slope form, then rewrite it in slope-intercept form: {{a}}

y − 4 = −2(x − 1)  →  y − 4 = −2x + 2  →  y = −2x + 6 - same answer.

{{/a}}

4.4.4Two Points Are Known

Steps
  1. Label the points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2).
  2. Find the slope: m = {{a}}{{f y2 − y1/x2 − x1}}{{/a}}
  3. Substitute {{a}}either one{{/a}} of the points into y = mx + b.
  4. Solve for b and write the equation.
EX 7. Through (1, 2) and (4, 5).

m = {{a}}1{{/a}}

b = {{a}}1{{/a}}    Equation: {{a}}y = x + 1{{/a}}

Check: does the other point fit your equation? {{a}}yes - 5 = 4 + 1 ✓{{/a}}

EX 8. Through (−2, 3) and (2, −1).
{{a}}

m = {{f −1 − 3/2 − (−2)}} = −1

3 = −1(−2) + b  →  b = 1, so y = −x + 1

{{/a}}
EX 9. Through (0, −2) and (3, 4).
{{a}}

m = {{f 4 − (−2)/3 − 0}} = 2, and b = −2 (the point is already on the y-axis)

y = 2x − 2

{{/a}}

4.4.5Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

Parallel lines have {{a}}equal{{/a}} slopes.

Perpendicular lines have {{a}}opposite reciprocal{{/a}} slopes: m⊥ = {{a}}−{{f 1/m}}{{/a}}
