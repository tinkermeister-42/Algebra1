5.4.1Factoring Out a GCF

Factoring is {{a}}the reverse of multiplying{{/a}}. Instead of expanding, we rewrite the expression as a {{a}}product{{/a}}.
The GCF is the largest number or expression that divides {{a}}every{{/a}} term.
EX 1. Factor 12x + 18.

12x breaks into {{a}}2 · 2 · 3 · x{{/a}}    18 breaks into {{a}}2 · 3 · 3{{/a}}

GCF = {{a}}6{{/a}}

12x + 18 = {{a}}6(2x + 3){{/a}}

12x3 = {{a}}3 · 4 · x2 · x{{/a}}    3x2 = {{a}}3 · x2{{/a}}

GCF = {{a}}3x2{{/a}}    Factored: {{a}}3x2(4x − 1){{/a}}

If every term shares a factor, take out the {{a}}GCF{{/a}} first, then use the area model on what is left.

Remember: the order of attack
  1. Put the polynomial in {{a}}standard form{{/a}}.
  2. Factor out a {{a}}GCF{{/a}} if there is one.
  3. If the first term is negative, include a {{a}}negative sign{{/a}} with the GCF.
  4. Use an area model on what remains.
  5. {{a}}Check{{/a}} your answer by multiplying it back out.
