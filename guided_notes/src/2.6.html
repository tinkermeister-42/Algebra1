2.6.1What Does “Equivalent” Mean?

Two expressions are equivalent if they give the same value for {{a}}every{{/a}} input. They do not have to {{a}}look{{/a}} the same.
EX 1. Compare A(x) = 3(x + 4) and B(x) = 3x + 12.
x3(x + 4)3x + 12
0{{a}}12{{/a}}{{a}}12{{/a}}
5{{a}}27{{/a}}{{a}}27{{/a}}

The outputs match. This is {{a}}evidence{{/a}} that they are equivalent.

Remember: evidence is not proof
Testing a few values gives {{a}}evidence{{/a}}. A proof shows it is true for {{a}}all{{/a}} inputs.
EX 2. Are C(x) = 4x + 1 and D(x) = 4(x + 1) equivalent? Try x = 2.

{{a}}

{{/a}}

A single value that makes the outputs differ is called a {{a}}counterexample{{/a}}.

2.6.2Proving Equivalence

We cannot test every value, so to prove equivalence we use {{a}}the properties of algebra{{/a}} to rewrite each expression until they {{a}}match{{/a}}.
The tools: {{a}}the distributive property{{/a}}, {{a}}combining like terms{{/a}}, and {{a}}factoring{{/a}}.
EX 5. Distribute. Prove 2(3 − y) and 6 − 2y are equivalent.
{{a}}

2(3 − y) = 6 − 2y ✓

{{/a}}
EX 6. Combine like terms. Prove 2x − 3y + 7 and 2 − 3x + 5 − 3y + 5x are equivalent.
{{a}}

2 − 3x + 5 − 3y + 5x

= (−3x + 5x) − 3y + (2 + 5) = 2x − 3y + 7 ✓

{{/a}}
EX 7. Simplify both sides. Prove 2(x − 1) + 3x and 2x + 3(x − 2) + 4 are equivalent.
{{a}}

2(x − 1) + 3x = 2x − 2 + 3x = 5x − 2

{{/a}}
{{a}}

2x + 3(x − 2) + 4 = 2x + 3x − 6 + 4 = 5x − 2

{{/a}}
EX 8. Factor. Prove 6z − 2 and 2(3z − 1) are equivalent.

The GCF of 6z and −2 is {{a}}2{{/a}}.

{{a}}

6z − 2 = 2(3z − 1) ✓

{{/a}}
EX 9. Disprove. Show 2x + 3 and 2(x + 3) are not equivalent, using algebra.
{{a}}

2(x + 3) = 2x + 6, and 2x + 6 ≠ 2x + 3 - the constants differ by 3 for every x.

{{/a}}
EX 10. Prove 3 − 2(x + 1) and x + 1 − 3x are equivalent.
{{a}}

3 − 2(x + 1) = 3 − 2x − 2 = 1 − 2x

{{/a}}
{{a}}

x + 1 − 3x = −2x + 1

{{/a}}

24. Find k so that 3(x + k) is equivalent to 3x + 12.
{{a}}

3(x + k) = 3x + 3k, so 3k = 12 and k = 4.

{{/a}}
25. Are these equivalent for all x? Prove it or give a counterexample.
E1(x) = (x + 2)(4x − 1) + 3(x + 2)    E2(x) = 2(x + 2)(2x + 1)
{{a}}

Factor (x + 2) out of E1:

E1 = (x + 2)[(4x − 1) + 3] = (x + 2)(4x + 2) = 2(x + 2)(2x + 1)

That is exactly E2, so they are equivalent for all x.
