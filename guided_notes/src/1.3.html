1.3.1Finding the GCF Using Factor Trees

  • Break both numbers into {{a}}prime factors{{/a}} with factor trees.
  • Circle the prime factors the two numbers {{a}}share{{/a}}.
  • {{a}}Multiply{{/a}} the circled factors together. That product is the GCF.
EX 1. Find the GCF of 36 and 60. Build a tree for each, then circle what they share.

36

60

Shared factors: {{a}}2 × 2 × 3{{/a}}    GCF = {{a}}12{{/a}}

{{a}}

36 = 22 × 32    60 = 22 × 3 × 5

{{/a}}
EX 2. Find the GCF of 45 and 180.

45

180

Shared factors: {{a}}3 × 3 × 5{{/a}}    GCF = {{a}}45{{/a}}

{{a}}

45 = 32 × 5    180 = 22 × 32 × 5

{{/a}}

1.3.2Using Factor Trees to Divide

When you find the GCF you do not circle every leaf. What is left behind is the {{a}}quotient{{/a}}.
36 ÷ 12 = {{a}}3{{/a}}     60 ÷ 12 = {{a}}5{{/a}}
EX 3. Use factor trees to compute 90 ÷ 15. Cross out the factors 90 shares with 15, then multiply what is left.

90

15

Left over: {{a}}2 × 3{{/a}}    90 ÷ 15 = {{a}}6{{/a}}

{{a}}

90 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 5    15 = 3 × 5    cross out 3 and 5

{{/a}}

480

60

1.3.3Simplifying Fractions with the GCF

To simplify a fraction, divide the {{a}}numerator{{/a}} and the {{a}}denominator{{/a}} by their {{a}}GCF{{/a}}.
A fraction is in simplest form when the top and bottom share no factor except {{a}}1{{/a}}.
EX 5. Simplify {{f 36/60}}   (you already found the GCF above)

{{f 36/60}} = {{f 36 ÷ ___/60 ÷ ___}} = {{a}}{{f 3/5}}{{/a}}

{{a}}

Both blanks are the GCF, 12.

{{/a}}
EX 6. Simplify {{f 90/15}}. What whole number does it equal?

{{f 90/15}} = {{a}}{{f 6/1}}{{/a}} = {{a}}6{{/a}}

Gotcha: what if they share no factors?
Then the two numbers are {{a}}relatively prime{{/a}} and the fraction {{a}}cannot{{/a}} be simplified.
Try it: simplify {{f 9/38}}  →  {{a}}{{f 9/38}} - already simplest{{/a}}
EX 7. Write {{f 30/90}} in simplest form.

GCF = {{a}}30{{/a}}     {{f 30/90}} = {{a}}{{f 1/3}}{{/a}}

Challenge

9. Two numbers multiply to make 180 and their GCF is 6. What could they be?
{{a}}

6 and 30 - 6 × 30 = 180 and their GCF is 6.

{{/a}}
10. A teacher has 150 pencils and 100 pens. Every gift bag has the same ratio of pencils to pens, and each bag has 2 more pencils than pens. She uses all the items. How many bags, and what goes in each?
{{a}}

150 : 100 = 3 : 2, so a bag holds 3k pencils and 2k pens. The difference k = 2, so each bag has 6 pencils and 4 pens - 150 ÷ 6 = 25 bags (and 100 ÷ 4 = 25 ✓).
