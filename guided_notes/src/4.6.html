4.6.1Solving Problems with Linear Models

Every linear model has two pieces.

The slope (m) tells how fast something {{a}}changes{{/a}}.

The y-intercept (b) tells where it {{a}}starts{{/a}}.

EX 1. Streaming. $2 per episode rented plus a $7.99 monthly fee.

Let e = {{a}}the number of episodes rented{{/a}} and P(e) = {{a}}the total cost for the month{{/a}}

m = {{a}}2{{/a}} because {{a}}each episode adds $2{{/a}}    b = {{a}}7.99{{/a}} because {{a}}the fee is charged even with 0 episodes{{/a}}

Model: P(e) = {{a}}2e + 7.99{{/a}}    Cost for 10 episodes: {{a}}$27.99{{/a}}

EX 2. Cooling soup. Soup cools from 90°C to 60°C in 10 minutes at a constant rate.

m = {{a}}−3{{/a}}    b = {{a}}90{{/a}}

Model: T(t) = {{a}}−3t + 90{{/a}}    Temperature after 15 minutes: {{a}}45°C{{/a}}

How much should I graph?
A model only makes sense for certain values. For the soup, times before {{a}}0{{/a}} would mean {{a}}before the soup was made{{/a}}, and after about 20 minutes the soup would be {{a}}colder than the room, which cannot happen{{/a}}. Graph only the part that fits the situation.
EX 3. Taxi. $3 to start plus $2.25 per mile.

Model: C(d) = {{a}}2.25d + 3{{/a}}    Cost of a 20-mile ride: {{a}}$48{{/a}}

EX 4. Comparing services. A ride-share charges no fee but $3.75 per mile. When is the taxi from EX 3 the better deal?

Ride-share model: R(d) = {{a}}3.75d{{/a}}

The lines cross near {{a}}2{{/a}} miles.

Break-even cost: {{a}}$7.50{{/a}}

For trips longer than that, {{a}}the taxi{{/a}} is cheaper.

EX 5. Babysitting. Estrella charges an hourly rate plus a minimum fee. She charged $70 for 5 hours and $46 for 3 hours.

Why can't we just divide money by hours? {{a}}there is a flat minimum fee on top of the hourly rate, so the total is not proportional to the hours{{/a}}

Rate (slope): m = {{a}}{{f 70 − 46/5 − 3}} = $12 per hour{{/a}}

Minimum fee: substitute a point and solve for b: {{a}}46 = 12(3) + b, so b = 10{{/a}}

Model: C(h) = {{a}}12h + 10{{/a}}    Charge for 12 hours: {{a}}$154{{/a}}

4.6.2Seeing Patterns in Data

Real data rarely lands in a perfect line, but it often shows a {{a}}trend{{/a}}. When that pattern looks roughly straight we draw a best-fit line.

Positive correlation: {{a}}as x goes up, y goes up{{/a}}

Negative correlation: {{a}}as x goes up, y goes down{{/a}}

No correlation: {{a}}no clear pattern{{/a}}

The closer the points are to the line, the {{a}}stronger{{/a}} the correlation.
EX 6. Study time vs. test score. The best-fit line is G(h) = 4.5h + 49.5.

What does the slope 4.5 mean? {{a}}each extra hour of study is worth about 4.5 more points{{/a}}

What does the intercept 49.5 mean? {{a}}the predicted score with no studying at all{{/a}}

Predicted score after 7 hours of study: {{a}}81{{/a}}

EX 7. Car weight vs. fuel economy. The best-fit line is F(w) = −0.007w + 70.

What does the negative slope mean? {{a}}heavier cars get fewer mpg — about 0.007 mpg less per pound{{/a}}

Does the intercept describe a real car? {{a}}no{{/a}} Why? {{a}}a car cannot weigh 0 lb{{/a}}

Predicted mpg for a 3450 lb car: {{a}}45.85 mpg{{/a}}
