3.1.1One-Step Equations: Addition and Subtraction

An equation is two expressions joined by an {{a}}equal sign{{/a}}. Think of a balance scale: whatever you do to one side you must do to the {{a}}other{{/a}}.
Our goal is to get the variable {{a}}by itself{{/a}}, and we do it with inverse operations.
EX 1. A scale holds an apple plus 2 weights on the left and 5 weights on the right. Find the weight a of the apple.
StepWhat I did to both sides
a + 2 = 5the equation
{{a}}a + 2 − 2 = 5 − 2{{/a}}{{a}}subtract 2 from both sides{{/a}}
{{a}}a = 3{{/a}}{{a}}simplify{{/a}}

We undo addition by {{a}}subtracting{{/a}}.

EX 2. Solve x + 9 = 11.
{{a}}

x + 9 − 9 = 11 − 9  →  x = 2

{{/a}}
EX 3. Solve x − 5 = 8.
x − 5 = 8the equation
{{a}}x − 5 + 5 = 8 + 5{{/a}}{{a}}add 5 to both sides{{/a}}
{{a}}x = 13{{/a}}{{a}}simplify{{/a}}

We undo subtraction by {{a}}adding{{/a}}.

EX 4. Solve x − 12 = 2.
{{a}}

x − 12 + 12 = 2 + 12  →  x = 14

{{/a}}

EX 5. Two apples balance 6 weights. Find the weight a of one apple.

3.1.3Checking a Solution

To check, put your answer back into the {{a}}original{{/a}} equation. If both sides come out {{a}}equal{{/a}}, you are right.

Check:{{a}}   3(7) = 21 ✓{{/a}}
