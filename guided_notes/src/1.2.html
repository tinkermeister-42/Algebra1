{{a}}

7 of them (1 through 7, since 7 × 7 = 49)

3. Can more than one pair of whole numbers multiply to make 13?

{{a}}

No — 13 is prime, so only 1 × 13

1.2.3Prime vs. Composite

  • A prime number has exactly {{a}}2{{/a}} factors: {{a}}1{{/a}} and {{a}}itself{{/a}}.
  • A composite number has {{a}}more{{/a}} than 2 factors.

1.2.4Prime Factorization & Factor Trees

Every number can be written as a product of {{a}}prime{{/a}} numbers. A factor tree is how we find them.
  1. Start with the number.
  2. Split it into any two numbers that {{a}}multiply{{/a}} to give it.
  3. Keep splitting until every branch ends in a {{a}}prime{{/a}} number (these ends are called {{a}}leaves{{/a}}).
  4. The prime factorization is the {{a}}product{{/a}} of the leaves.
EX 5. Build a factor tree for 360 starting with 360 = 18 × 20.

Prime factorization: {{a}}2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 5{{/a}}

Written with exponents: {{a}}23 × 32 × 5{{/a}}

{{a}}

18 = 2 × 3 × 3    20 = 2 × 2 × 5

{{/a}}

Gotcha — which factors should I start with?
There is no single right answer. Starting with the smallest factors makes a {{a}}taller, skinnier{{/a}} tree; starting with a pair close together makes a {{a}}shorter, wider{{/a}} tree. Either way the prime factorization is {{a}}the same either way{{/a}}.

Prime factorization of −24: {{a}}−1 × 23 × 3{{/a}}

Challenge

6. Can two different numbers have the same prime factorization? Why or why not?
{{a}}

No. Every number has exactly one prime factorization, so the same set of primes always multiplies back to the same single number.
