6.7  Applications of Quadratics

Guided Notes - Unit 6: Quadratic Equations & Functions - DHS Algebra 1
Teacher Key: answers in red
Objectives
  • Write a quadratic equation to model a real situation
  • Solve quadratic application problems
  • Interpret solutions in context

Warm-Up

1. Why would we use a quadratic to model a ball thrown in the air?

Gravity makes the height rise and then fall. That path is a parabola.

2. If a quadratic has two solutions, what might they represent in a real situation?

Two moments when the quantity hits that value, for example on the way up and on the way down, or two break-even points.

3. When would only one of the two solutions make sense?

When the other is negative (time, length or a count cannot be negative) or otherwise impossible in context.

6.7.1Quadratics as Models

Because parabolas curve, quadratic models are useful when something rises and then falls, increases and then decreases, or has a maximum or minimum value.
EX 1. Using features of a parabola in real situations.
FeatureWhat it tells usExample
VertexThe highest or lowest value of the situationMaximum height of a ball, highest profit, minimum cost
y-interceptThe starting value when x = 0Starting height, initial amount of money, starting population
x-interceptsWhen the quantity becomes zeroWhen an object hits the ground, when profit is zero, break-even points
Axis of symmetryThe input where the situation switches directionWhen something stops increasing and starts decreasing
Opening directionWhether the situation has a maximum or minimumDownward opening for max height, upward opening for min cost
EX 2. Which feature would you use?

a. A ball is thrown from a cliff. How tall is the cliff? the y-intercept

b. A skateboarder rides a ramp. How long to reach the bottom? the x-coordinate of the vertex

c. A business sells shirts. How many to break even? the x-intercepts

d. A rocket is fired up. How high does it go? the y-coordinate of the vertex

6.7.2Projectile Motion

An object under gravity is modeled by h(t) = at2 + bt + c, where t is time in seconds and h is height in feet.
EX 3. A ball is thrown upward from 5 feet: h(t) = −16t2 + 40t + 5.

Part 1: When does it hit the ground? Set h(t) = 0, because the ball hits the ground when its height is zero.

Solve with the quadratic formula:

t = −40 ± √1920−32, and √1920 = 8√30 ≈ 43.82

Two answers: t ≈ 2.62 and t ≈ −0.12. We reject the negative one because time cannot be negative.

The ball lands after about 2.62 seconds.

Gotcha: interpreting solutions
Quadratic equations often produce two solutions. In applications, you must decide which solutions make sense in context. Negative time, negative length, or negative area usually do not apply.
EX 4. Same ball. What is its maximum height?

The maximum happens at the vertex, so t = −b2a = −40−32 = 1.25

Maximum height = h(1.25) = 30 feet

EX 5. A ball is thrown from 6 feet with h(t) = −16t2 + 24t + 6. When does it hit the ground?

0 = −16t2 + 24t + 6; disc = 576 + 384 = 960

t = −24 ± √960−32; the positive root is t ≈ 1.72 seconds.

6.7.3Area Problems

EX 6. A rectangular garden is 4 feet longer than it is wide and has area 96 square feet. Find its dimensions.

Let x = the width in feet, so the length is x + 4.

Equation: x(x + 4) = 96  →  standard form: x2 + 4x − 96 = 0

Solutions: (x + 12)(x − 8) = 0, so x = 8 or −12    We reject −12 because a width cannot be negative.

Width 8 ft, length 12 ft

EX 7. A pool has a 2-foot walkway all the way around. Pool plus walkway is 192 square feet, and the pool is 4 feet longer than it is wide. Find the pool's dimensions.

Let w = pool width, so pool length = w + 4.

The walkway adds 4 feet to each dimension, so the outside is w + 4 by w + 8.

Equation: (w + 4)(w + 8) = 192 → w2 + 12w − 160 = 0

Pool: 8 ft by 12 ft

6.7.4Revenue and Profit

EX 8. Tickets cost $10 and 80 are sold. Each $1 increase loses 5 sales. Find the price that maximizes revenue.

Let x be the number of $1 increases.

Price: 10 + x    Tickets sold: 80 − 5x

Revenue: R(x) = (10 + x)(80 − 5x) = −5x2 + 30x + 800

Vertex at x = 3, so the price is $13, tickets sold 65, and maximum revenue $845.

EX 9. A food truck sells 60 lunches at $8. Each $1 increase loses 4 sales. Write the revenue equation and find the best price.

Let x be the number of $1 price increases.

Careful: the vertex is not a whole dollar. What do you do then? check the whole-dollar prices on either side, x = 3 and x = 4

R(x) = (8 + x)(60 − 4x) = −4x2 + 28x + 480

Vertex x = 3.5. x counts $1 increases, so check x = 3 and x = 4: both give $528.

Best price: $11 or $12.

6.7 Applications of Quadratics - Practice

Practice

Level 1 - Understanding the Model

1. Which part of a quadratic gives the maximum or minimum value? the vertex

2. What does a solution of h(t) = 0 usually represent in a height problem? the moment the object reaches the ground (height 0)

3. Why do some quadratic solutions get rejected in real problems? negative times, lengths or counts do not make sense in context

Level 2 - Applications

4. A ball is thrown up from the ground with h(t) = −16t2 + 32t. When does it hit the ground?

Answer: t = 2 seconds

−16t(t − 2) = 0 → t = 0 or 2

5. A rectangle has area 40 square feet and its length is 3 feet longer than its width. Find the dimensions.

Answer: 5 ft by 8 ft

x(x+3) = 40 → (x+8)(x−5) = 0

6. A firework's height is h(t) = −16t2 + 48t + 10. What is its maximum height?

Answer: 46 feet, at t = 1.5 s

7. Profit from selling x items is P(x) = −x2 + 20x − 60. How many items maximize profit?

Answer: 10 items

Level 3 - Challenging

8. A ball is thrown up from a 40-foot cliff: h(t) = −16t2 + 16t + 40. When does it hit the ground?

−16t2 + 16t + 40 = 0; divide by −8: 2t2 − 2t − 5 = 0

t = 1 ± √112; the positive root is ≈ 2.16 seconds.

9. A rectangle has perimeter 40 units. If the width is x, show the length must be 20 − x. Then find the x that maximizes area.

2x + 2L = 40, so L = 20 − x.

A = x(20 − x) = −x2 + 20x, with vertex at x = 10.

A 10 × 10 square, area 100. This is the largest possible area.

10. A quadratic model has zeros at t = 1 and t = 5. What might those represent in a real situation?

The two times the quantity is zero, for example an object leaving the ground at t = 1 and landing at t = 5.

Challenge

11. Write a real-world situation that could be modeled by y = −2x2 + 12x − 5. Explain what the vertex represents.

Answers vary. For example: profit in hundreds of dollars from selling x hundred items.

The vertex is (3, 13), so selling 300 items gives the maximum profit, $1300.

12. A projectile reaches a maximum height of 64 feet after 2 seconds and lands at t = 5. Write a possible quadratic model.

Vertex (2, 64), so h(t) = a(t − 2)2 + 64.

At t = 5: 9a + 64 = 0 → a = −649

h(t) = −649(t − 2)2 + 64
