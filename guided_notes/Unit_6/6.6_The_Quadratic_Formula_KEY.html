(x + 3)2 = 4, so x = −1 or −5

It does not factor, but completing the square still works: x = −5 ± √212

3. Three equations were solved by completing the square. What pattern do you see in where the numbers end up?

x2 + 4x − 1 = 0 ⇒ x = −4 ± √202
2x2 − 6x + 1 = 0 ⇒ x = 6 ± √284
x2 − 2x − 4 = 0 ⇒ x = 2 ± √202

Every answer has the shape −b ± √(b2 − 4ac)2a - that is the quadratic formula.

6.6.1Deriving the Quadratic Formula

Start with the most general quadratic there is: ax2 + bx + c = 0, where a ≠ 0. Solve that, and you can solve any quadratic.
EX 1. Complete the square on the general equation.

The missing corner is b24a2

The quadratic formula:   x = −b ± √(b2 − 4ac)2a

6.6.2Using the Formula

The steps never change
  1. Identify a, b, and c. The equation must be in standard form first.
  2. Substitute carefully, using parentheses.
  3. Simplify step by step.
EX 2. Solve x2 + 3x + 1 = 0.

a = 1   b = 3   c = 1

Solutions: x = −3 ± √52 ≈ −0.38 or −2.62

This one does not factor, but the formula still gives exact answers.

EX 3. Solve 2x2 − 5x + 2 = 0.

a = 2   b = −5   c = 2

Solutions: disc = 9, so x = 5 ± 34 = 2 or 12

EX 4. Solve 3x2 + 4x + 7 = 0.

What went wrong under the square root? the discriminant is 16 − 84 = −68, and you cannot take the square root of a negative

So this equation has no real solutions.

EX 5. Solve 4x2 − 5x − 12 = 0.

a = 4, b = −5, c = −12; disc = 25 + 192 = 217

x = 5 ± √2178 ≈ 2.47 or −1.22

6.6.3The Discriminant

The discriminant is the part under the square root: b2 − 4ac. It tells you what kind of solutions you will get before you finish solving.
DiscriminantNumber of real solutions What the graph does
b2 − 4ac > 0twocrosses the x-axis twice
b2 − 4ac = 0onetouches the x-axis at the vertex
b2 − 4ac < 0nonenever touches the x-axis
EX 6. Find the discriminant and the number of solutions.

Is it factorable?
If the discriminant is a perfect square, the quadratic factors over the integers.
Check 2x2 + 5x − 3: discriminant = 49, which is a perfect square, so it factors as (2x − 1)(x + 3).
